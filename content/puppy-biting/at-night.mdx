Many owners and trainers report a predictable evening spike in puppy biting, restlessness, and general chaos — sometimes called the "witching hour," a piece of common owner/trainer terminology rather than a medical or veterinary term.<sup>1</sup> The most commonly reported explanation is a combination of accumulated stimulation over the day and insufficient rest, but it's worth being direct about the evidence here: **this is a widely observed pattern, not something we found established as a confirmed cause by a veterinary or board-certified behaviorist source.** Treat overtiredness/overstimulation as a plausible, worth-checking factor — not a certainty.

## What's Commonly Reported (and What Isn't Established)

- **Commonly reported by trainers:** evening biting spikes often coincide with a long day of activity and less structured rest, and puppies who get more consistent daytime naps often show less evening chaos.
- **Genuinely established by veterinary sources:** "arousal" and impulsivity are recognized concepts in veterinary behavioral medicine generally,<sup>2</sup> and ruling out pain or a medical cause is a standard first step before assuming any behavior is purely about tiredness or excitement.<sup>2</sup>
- **Not established as fact:** a specific causal link between accumulated daytime stimulation and evening biting timing. It's a plausible, frequently observed pattern — treat it as a reasonable first thing to check, not a confirmed mechanism.

## What to Try Tonight

1. **Check the day's rest, not just activity.** If naps were skipped or interrupted, an earlier, calmer wind-down tonight is a reasonable first thing to try — see the [Daily Puppy Schedule](/puppy-schedule/) for how rest fits into the rest of the day.
2. **Lower the stimulation, don't add more.** More vigorous play to "burn it off" often makes evening biting worse rather than better, per common trainer observation — a calm chew toy and quiet time is usually the better bet than another round of fetch.
3. **Use the standard biting response.** Stop movement, calmly disengage, redirect to an appropriate toy — the same default response used everywhere else on this site (see [Puppy Biting Hands](/puppy-biting/hands/)), not a special nighttime technique.<sup>3</sup>
4. **Start the wind-down earlier than feels necessary.** Lower the household's energy 30–60 minutes before the final potty break and bedtime, rather than going from full play straight into the [crate](/crate-training/).
5. **Track it for a few nights.** If evening biting consistently follows short-nap days and eases on well-rested days, that's useful information specific to your puppy — even without a confirmed general mechanism.

## If Tonight's Approach Doesn't Help

If a calmer, better-rested evening routine doesn't reduce the pattern over a week or two, or if the behavior seems to go beyond typical mouthing (real aggression signals, injury-level biting, or behavior that doesn't fit the usual play pattern), that's a reasonable point to involve a certified trainer, behavior consultant, or veterinary behaviorist rather than continuing to guess at home.
