<AnswerFirstSummary>
  A sudden increase almost always has an identifiable trigger — most commonly a teething flare-up, a rest/schedule disruption, a routine change, or (less often) pain — and identifying which one applies changes what helps.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Common Triggers

- **Teething flare-up** — different teeth erupt at different points over months (see [Puppy Teething Timeline](/puppy-teething/timeline/)).
- **Rest/schedule disruption** — see [Puppy Sleep](/puppy-sleep/); "overtiredness causes biting" remains a commonly reported pattern, not a proven mechanism.
- **Recent environmental/routine change.**
- **Reduced consistency** in how biting is handled.
- **Pain or illness** — less common, but real.

## What to Do

1. Check for teething signs.
2. Review recent household consistency.
3. Check recent sleep/rest disruption.
4. Consider other recent changes.
5. If nothing behavioral explains it, talk to your vet.

## Troubleshooting

| What changed | Likely trigger | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Puppy is 12 weeks–6 months | Possible teething flare-up | See [Puppy Teething](/puppy-teething/); ensure chew outlets |
| Recent nap/schedule disruption | Rest-related, commonly reported | Re-establish consistent rest |
| Recent household/environment change | Adjustment | Re-tighten consistency |
| Redirection lapsed recently | Consistency gap | Get everyone aligned |
| No identifiable trigger or other unusual signs | Possible medical cause | Talk to your veterinarian |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming earlier progress "didn't work."
- Escalating technique out of frustration.
- Not considering rest first.
- Ignoring an unexplained sudden change.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

A sudden increase paired with stiffer, more intense body language rather than just frequency — see [Is My Puppy Being Aggressive, or Just Playing?](/puppy-biting/aggression-or-play/) Paired with illness signs — see your vet.
