## Why There's No Precise Answer

Individual variation is real, and how consistently biting is handled genuinely affects the timeline — this isn't purely a fixed developmental clock.

## What's Actually Known

The AKC's Chief Veterinarian names ~6 months without moderation as a reasonable checkpoint for professional help<sup>1</sup> — a "worth checking in" point, not a promise. Most puppies show gradual decline in frequency and intensity well before that, especially with consistent handling.

## What "Improvement" Usually Looks Like

Softer bites before they fade entirely; longer stretches between incidents; more self-interruption; gradual context-specific easing (calm play improves before high-excitement moments do). Worth knowing so you're not judging against an all-or-nothing standard.

## If Consistent Handling Isn't Producing This Trend

See [Puppy Won't Stop Biting Despite Redirection](/puppy-biting/wont-stop/) rather than just waiting longer.

## What to Expect, Roughly

| Timeframe | Reasonable general expectation |
|---|---|
| Early weeks, consistent handling | Frequent mouthing, gradual softening |
| Several weeks to a few months in | Longer stretches, more self-interruption |
| ~6 months, with consistent handling | Noticeable improvement expected; if not, professional input is reasonable<sup>1</sup> |

*Individual variation is real — general pattern, not a guarantee.*

## Common Mistakes

- Expecting a sudden complete stop at a specific age.
- Judging only by "has it completely stopped."
- Waiting well past 6 months of no improvement before considering help.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

No improvement despite consistent, correct handling over several months; any shift toward the concerning signs in [Is My Puppy Being Aggressive, or Just Playing?](/puppy-biting/aggression-or-play/)
