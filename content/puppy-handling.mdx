  The goal is building genuine comfort with the touch and restraint your dog will need throughout life — vet exams, nail trims, brushing, medication — using gradual, reward-based exposure, never force.

## Why This Matters Beyond Grooming

A dog comfortable being handled has an easier time at every vet visit and grooming appointment, and every moment you need to check them for an injury.

## The Core Method, in Brief

1. Start with brief, gentle touch, paired with something your puppy likes.
2. Stop before discomfort shows, not after.
3. Gradually increase duration and intensity as each step stays comfortable.
4. Never force through resistance — back up a step instead.
5. Practice regularly, not just before a stressful event.

This shape applies across every body part and care type — see the spokes below.

## Getting Started

- [ ] Pick one body part or care type to start with this week
- [ ] Have a reward ready before you start
- [ ] Keep sessions brief — a minute or two, several times, beats one long session
- [ ] Stop at the first sign of discomfort

## Common Mistakes

- Only practicing right before an appointment.
- Pushing through resistance.
- Treating one success as "done."

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Fear that doesn't ease with a slow approach, or resistance tied to pain — slow down and involve a professional.
