<AnswerFirstSummary>
  There's no precisely correct number of minutes — favor moderate, varied, mostly self-directed activity over forced, repetitive, high-impact exercise, and watch your individual puppy's response rather than a fixed formula.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why This Page Won't Give You a Precise Number

A popular rule ("five minutes per month of age, twice daily") isn't traceable to a veterinary orthopedic source — see [The Five-Minute Rule](/puppy-exercise/five-minute-rule/) for the full review.

## What's Actually Well-Supported

- Favor moderate, varied, self-directed activity over forced, repetitive, high-impact activity.
- Physical and mental activity are both real needs — see [Exercise vs. Mental Stimulation](/puppy-exercise/vs-mental-stimulation/).
- Watch your individual puppy, not a formula.

## Quick Reference

| Favor | Use caution with |
|---|---|
| Moderate walks, varied terrain | Forced long-distance running |
| Free play, self-directed movement | Repeated high jumps onto/off hard surfaces |
| Sniffing and exploration | Excessive stair repetition, especially in larger breeds |
| Rest built in between activity | Back-to-back high-intensity sessions with no recovery |

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a viral formula as medical fact.
- Assuming more exercise is always better.
- Ignoring individual signals.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Limping, reluctance, yelping during/after activity, or sudden movement changes.
