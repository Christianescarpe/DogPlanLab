## Distance Is Your Main Tool

Find the distance at which your puppy can see another dog and stay reasonably calm — this is your starting point, even if that distance feels surprisingly large at first.

## The Recovery Approach

1. At a comfortable distance, reward calm behavior when your puppy notices another dog.
2. Don't approach closer than your puppy can handle — retreat if needed rather than pushing through.
3. Practice repeatedly at that distance before decreasing it, even slightly.
4. Skip forced greetings entirely during this phase — a scared puppy doesn't need to "meet" the dog to make progress; they need to get comfortable at a distance first.

## If the Fear Followed a Specific Incident

If a specific bad experience with another dog caused this (a scary encounter, being bullied or attacked), see [Bad Experience](/puppy-socialization/bad-experience/) for guidance specifically built around recovering from a known triggering event.

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing a greeting to "get past it."
- Taking a fearful puppy to a dog park hoping exposure will help — it's much more likely to make things worse.
- Punishing fear-based reactions like barking or retreating.

If fear seems specific to certain types of dogs (size, breed, energy level) rather than all dogs, tailor your distance-based practice to that category specifically rather than generic exposure.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If fear is intense, doesn't improve with distance-based practice over several weeks, or includes growling/snapping/lunging rather than just avoidance, a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist should be involved — this pattern can also affect safety, not just comfort.
