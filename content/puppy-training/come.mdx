<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Build recall in easy stages — starting close, with no distraction, and a big reward every time — before ever testing it somewhere your puppy could actually get away from you. The single most important rule: never punish or scold your puppy for coming to you, even if it took a while.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Start Ridiculously Easy

Begin a few feet away in a quiet room. Say your recall word once ("come" or "here" — pick one and stick with it), and reward generously the moment your puppy arrives.

## Build Distance and Distraction Separately

Increase distance first, in the same low-distraction setting, before adding any real-world distraction. Only once your puppy is reliable at a real distance indoors should you start practicing with mild distractions, then more challenging ones — ideally on a long leash outdoors, not off-leash, until recall is genuinely solid.

## Make Coming to You the Best Available Option

Your puppy is choosing between whatever they're doing and coming to you — that choice is only easy if coming to you is clearly worth it. A better, higher-value reward for recall specifically (not just any treat) helps make that an easy choice.

## The One Rule That Matters Most

**Never punish, scold, or express frustration when your puppy finally comes to you** — even if it took much longer than you wanted, even if they got into something they shouldn't have first. Punishing an arrival teaches a puppy that coming to you is risky, which actively damages recall for the long run.

## Building Recall in Stages

| Stage | Setting | Distance | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quiet room | A few feet | Instant, reliable response |
| 2 | Quiet room | Across the room | Reliable at real distance |
| 3 | Quiet room | Across the room | Reliable with mild distraction (a toy nearby) |
| 4 | Yard/quiet outdoor space, on a long leash | Moderate | Reliable outdoors with real smells/sounds |
| 5 | More distracting outdoor space, still leashed | Increasing | Reliable approaching real-world conditions |

## What to Do Today

Practice Stage 1 a few times in a quiet room with a high-value reward. If you're already further along, check whether you've skipped a stage rather than assuming your puppy is "just not listening."

## Common Mistakes

- Testing recall somewhere your puppy could actually run off before it's reliable.
- Calling repeatedly if the first call doesn't work — teaches that the first call doesn't matter.
- Using "come" to call your puppy for something unpleasant (nail trim, bath) without separately making it worth it.
- Punishing a slow or reluctant arrival.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If recall isn't improving despite consistent short-session practice over a few weeks, or if your puppy shows signs of fear or avoidance specifically around being called, a certified trainer can help troubleshoot what's actually happening.
