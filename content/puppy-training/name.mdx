<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Your puppy's name should function as an attention-getter — "look at me, something's coming" — not as a command in itself. Teach it by saying the name, marking the instant your puppy looks at you, and rewarding immediately.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Name vs. Command

Keeping these distinct matters: the name means "check in with me," while a specific cue (like "watch me") can be taught separately for sustained eye contact. This distinction keeps the name useful as a quick attention-getter rather than something your puppy tunes out from overuse.

## How to Teach It

1. Say your puppy's name once, in a normal tone (not repeated, not shouted).
2. The instant your puppy looks at you, mark the moment (a clear word like "yes!" or a clicker) and reward immediately.
3. Repeat in short bursts, in a low-distraction setting first.
4. Gradually practice with mild distractions, then more challenging ones, before expecting reliable response in an exciting environment.

## Timing Matters

The mark should come within about half a second of your puppy looking at you, with the reward following immediately after. A delay of even a second or two can blur what's actually being rewarded.

## Checklist

- [ ] Say the name once, not repeatedly
- [ ] Mark the instant of eye contact
- [ ] Reward immediately after the mark
- [ ] Practice in a quiet space before adding distraction
- [ ] Don't use the name for corrections — it should stay a purely positive cue

## What to Do Today

Run 3–5 short name-and-mark reps in a quiet room. Resist repeating the name if your puppy doesn't respond immediately — instead, get closer or reduce distraction and try again.

## Common Mistakes

- Repeating the name over and over until the puppy responds — teaches that the name can be ignored the first few times.
- Using the name right before something unpleasant (vet nail trim, bath) without pairing it back to something positive.
- Expecting instant reliability around exciting distractions before building up to them.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If your puppy consistently doesn't orient to sound or seems not to notice their name even up close in a quiet room, that's worth mentioning to your veterinarian to rule out a hearing-related cause before assuming it's purely a training issue.
