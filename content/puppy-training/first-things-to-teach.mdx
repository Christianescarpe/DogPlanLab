<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Start with attention (name recognition), not obedience commands — a puppy that can't focus on you can't reliably learn anything else. From there: sit, come, leave it, and drop it form the core early skill set, roughly in that order, though your puppy's actual progress should drive the pace more than a fixed sequence.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Attention Comes First

Every other skill depends on your puppy being able to focus on you, at least briefly, in the presence of distraction. Skipping straight to "sit" or "come" without this foundation tends to produce a puppy who only responds in a quiet room — see [Name & Attention](/puppy-training/name/).

## A Practical Order

1. **Name & attention** — the foundation everything else builds on.
2. **Sit** — simple, fast to teach, useful as a default "polite" behavior (see [Sit, Down & Stay](/puppy-training/sit-down-stay/)).
3. **Come** — high-value, safety-relevant; easier to build well early than to fix later (see [Come](/puppy-training/come/)).
4. **Leave it** — safety-critical; prevents your puppy from grabbing something dangerous (see [Leave It](/puppy-training/leave-it/)).
5. **Drop it** — safety-critical for the cases leave it didn't catch in time (see [Drop It](/puppy-training/drop-it/)).
6. **Down & stay** — building on sit, useful for calmer moments (see [Sit, Down & Stay](/puppy-training/sit-down-stay/)).
7. **Loose-leash walking** — once basic attention is solid (see [Leash Walking & Equipment](/puppy-training/leash/)).

This is a practical starting order, not a rigid rule — some puppies pick up "come" faster than "sit," and that's fine. Follow your puppy's actual progress.

## Don't Try to Teach Everything at Once

Layering three new skills into one session is a common mistake. Pick one, get a handful of short, successful sessions in, then add the next.

## Skill, Purpose, Roughly When

| Skill | Purpose | Roughly When |
|---|---|---|
| Name & attention | Foundation for everything else | Day one |
| Sit | Simple, useful default behavior | Week 1 |
| Come | Safety, recall | Week 1–2 |
| Leave it | Prevent grabbing dangerous items | Week 1–2 |
| Drop it | Release items already grabbed | Week 2–3 |
| Down & stay | Builds on sit, calmer moments | Week 2–4 |
| Loose-leash walking | Once attention is reasonably solid | Week 3+ |

## What to Do Today

If you haven't started, begin with a short name/attention session. If you have, check whether you're layering too many new skills at once.

## Common Mistakes

- Starting with obedience commands before attention.
- Teaching too many new skills simultaneously.
- Abandoning a skill after one bad session instead of shortening and simplifying it.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If your puppy struggles to make any progress on attention specifically, even in a low-distraction setting, after consistent short sessions over a couple of weeks, a certified trainer can help identify what's getting in the way.
