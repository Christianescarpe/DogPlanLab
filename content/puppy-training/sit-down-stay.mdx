<AnswerFirstSummary>
  All three build on the same lure-and-reward mechanic and a natural progression — sit first, then down (built from sit), then stay (built by adding duration and distance gradually to either).
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Sit

1. Hold a treat close to your puppy's nose, then move it slowly up and back over their head.
2. As their head tilts up to follow it, their rear naturally lowers into a sit.
3. Mark the instant their rear touches the ground, then reward.
4. Repeat until reliable, then add the cue word just before the motion.

## Down

1. From a sit, hold a treat at your puppy's nose, then lower it straight to the floor.
2. As your puppy follows the treat down, their body should lower with it — reward the moment their elbows touch the ground.
3. If they pop back up instead of lying down, you're likely moving the treat too fast — slow down.

## Stay

"Stay" isn't a new position — it's duration and distance added to sit or down.

1. Ask for a sit or down your puppy is already reliable at.
2. Wait one second, then mark and reward while your puppy is still in position.
3. Gradually increase the wait time, in small increments — a few seconds at a time, not minutes right away.
4. Once duration is solid, add small amounts of distance (a half-step back) before returning to reward.
5. Build duration and distance separately, not both at once — if you increase both simultaneously, you're likely to see the behavior fall apart.

## A Note on Attention Span

These are short, achievable sessions — a young puppy building real reliability on "stay" over multiple weeks, with duration measured in single-digit seconds at first, is completely normal progress, not a sign anything's wrong.

## Building Stay Duration

| Stage | Duration | Distance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 seconds | None |
| 2 | 5–10 seconds | None |
| 3 | 5–10 seconds | Half-step |
| 4 | 10–20 seconds | 1–2 steps |
| 5 | Building gradually from there | Building gradually from there |

## What to Do Today

Practice sit with the lure method for a few reps. If sit is solid, try down. If both are solid, add 2–3 seconds of stay duration before releasing.

## Common Mistakes

- Moving the treat too fast during the down lure — causes puppies to pop back up.
- Increasing duration and distance for stay at the same time.
- Expecting long stays before short ones are solid.
- Releasing your puppy from a stay by calling them — this can confuse stay with come; use a separate release word.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If your puppy seems physically uncomfortable getting into a down position (reluctant, stiff, or seems to avoid it specifically), that's worth a vet check before continuing to push the behavior, in case there's a physical cause rather than a training gap.
