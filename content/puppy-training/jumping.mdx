## Why Jumping Works (From Your Puppy's Perspective)

Dogs jump to greet people and get their attention. Owners typically respond by talking, pushing the dog down, or otherwise touching them — all of which the puppy experiences as attention, which reinforces the jumping rather than reducing it.<sup>1</sup>

## The Fix: Remove the Reward, Reward the Alternative

1. The instant your puppy jumps, become boring: no talking, no eye contact, no touching — turn away or stand completely still.<sup>1</sup>
2. The moment all four paws are on the floor (or your puppy sits), immediately reward with attention, praise, and/or a treat.
3. Practice this consistently — including with guests, who need to be briefed on the same approach, or they'll accidentally reinforce jumping right when you're trying hardest to prevent it.
4. For a puppy that jumps the second a door opens, practice greetings in low-key, controlled repetitions before expecting it to hold up with an excited real arrival.

## Managing Guests

Ask visitors to ignore jumping and only greet your puppy once all four paws are on the ground — most people are happy to help once they understand why. If that's not realistic in the moment, a brief management step (a leash, a gate) to prevent the jump from happening at all is a reasonable bridge while training is still in progress.

## Do This / Not This

| Situation | Do This | Not This |
|---|---|---|
| Puppy jumps on you | Turn away, go still, no talking | Push them down, say "no," or otherwise engage |
| Puppy has all four paws down | Reward immediately with attention/treats | Wait to see if they jump again first |
| Guest arrives and puppy jumps | Brief guests in advance; manage with a leash if needed | Let guests pet/talk to a jumping puppy "just this once" |
| Puppy jumps out of excitement, not just greeting | Same principle — remove attention, reward calm | Assume excitement-jumping needs a different (harsher) approach |

## What to Do Today

Practice one calm entrance/exit from a room, rewarding four-on-the-floor. Brief anyone in the household on the same approach so it's consistent.

## Common Mistakes

- Inconsistent responses — ignoring jumping sometimes, engaging with it other times.
- Guests accidentally reinforcing jumping because they weren't briefed.
- Scolding, which still counts as attention from your puppy's perspective.
- Expecting the behavior to change without addressing the actual reward driving it.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If jumping is paired with signs of anxiety or overarousal that don't improve with consistent practice, or if the behavior seems to be escalating rather than improving, a certified trainer can help.
