<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Teach "drop it" by trading — offering something better in exchange for what your puppy has — never by prying their mouth open or chasing them down, both of which teach a puppy to run or guard rather than release willingly.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Trading, Not Taking

Chasing a puppy to retrieve something, or forcing their mouth open, teaches exactly the wrong lesson: that giving something up means losing it forever with no upside, which makes a puppy more likely to run, gulp the item down, or guard it next time. Trading teaches the opposite: giving something up leads to something good.

## How to Teach It

1. Offer your puppy a low-value toy — something they'll hold but aren't obsessed with.
2. Present a high-value treat near their nose and say "drop it."
3. The instant they release the toy to investigate the treat, mark and reward, then give the toy back sometimes (so dropping doesn't always mean losing the item forever).
4. Repeat with gradually higher-value items as your puppy gets reliable.
5. If your puppy tries to keep the item and dodge you, don't chase — toss a treat a few feet away to create space and buy a moment, or scatter a few treats to redirect attention.

## Practicing Ahead of Real Emergencies

Practice "drop it" regularly with safe items so it's a well-rehearsed behavior before you ever need it for something genuinely dangerous — a puppy's first experience with the cue shouldn't be a high-stakes moment.

## Do This / Not This

| Situation | Do This | Not This |
|---|---|---|
| Puppy has a toy | Trade for a treat, say "drop it" | Pry the mouth open |
| Puppy grabs something and runs | Toss a treat away to create space, or scatter treats | Chase your puppy |
| Puppy drops the item | Give the toy back sometimes | Always confiscate — this teaches dropping = losing forever |
| Puppy has something genuinely dangerous | Trade with the highest-value reward you have | Yell or grab at the puppy's face |

## What to Do Today

Practice one trade-based rep with a low-value toy. If your puppy tends to run when you approach something they have, start working on this now rather than waiting for a real emergency.

## Common Mistakes

- Only ever taking the item and never giving anything back — teaches "drop it" predicts loss.
- Chasing a puppy who runs off with something.
- Practicing for the first time in a real emergency instead of low-stakes situations.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

Growling, snapping, or stiffening when you approach an item — rather than simple reluctance — is a resource-guarding signal distinct from ordinary "doesn't want to give it up," and is worth a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist's input.
