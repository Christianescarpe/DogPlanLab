<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Teething happens in two separate phases — baby teeth erupting in the first weeks of life, then permanent teeth replacing them starting around 12 weeks — and increased chewing/mouthing is a normal, expected part of both phases, especially the second. What changes the answer: which phase your puppy is in changes what you'll actually see; a full timeline (including specific tooth types) is on the [Teething Timeline](/puppy-teething/timeline/) page.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## What Teething Actually Looks Like

Increased chewing and mouthing on objects is the most common visible sign of teething — it's understood to help relieve gum discomfort as teeth erupt.<sup>1</sup> You may also notice a stronger "puppy breath" smell and, later on, tiny teeth around the house as baby teeth fall out (many puppies also swallow them, which is normal and not a concern).

## The Two Phases, Briefly

- **Baby teeth (deciduous) erupting:** happens very early, mostly complete well before most puppies come home with a new owner.
- **Permanent teeth erupting, baby teeth falling out:** starts around 12 weeks and continues to around 6 months — this second phase is generally the more intense, more chewing-heavy one.<sup>2</sup>

See [Puppy Teething Timeline](/puppy-teething/timeline/) for the full week-by-week and tooth-type breakdown.

## How to Help

- Offer appropriate chew toys, and rotate them for variety.
- Puppy-proof reachable areas rather than relying on your puppy to know what's off-limits.
- Redirect to an appropriate chew item when you catch inappropriate chewing, rather than punishing after the fact.
- See [Puppy Chewing Everything](/puppy-chewing/everything/) if chewing is becoming a bigger management problem than typical teething discomfort explains.

## Not Every Chewing Problem Is "Just Teething"

Teething is one real contributor to increased chewing, but boredom, insufficient exercise/mental stimulation, exploration, and (less often) anxiety are all separate, common contributors too<sup>3</sup> — see [Puppy Chewing Everything](/puppy-chewing/everything/) for the full breakdown. Don't assume a chewing problem will fully resolve once teething ends if the underlying driver is actually one of these other factors.

## Is This Normal Teething?

- [ ] Increased chewing/mouthing on objects (not primarily on people — see [Puppy Biting](/puppy-biting/) if it's mostly directed at hands/feet)
- [ ] "Puppy breath" or mild gum redness
- [ ] Age-appropriate for one of the two eruption phases (see the [Timeline](/puppy-teething/timeline/))
- [ ] No excessive bleeding, drooling, facial swelling, or refusal to eat

If the last box doesn't check, see "When to Call Your Veterinarian" below.

## What to Do Today

1. Make sure appropriate chew toys are genuinely accessible, not just owned.
2. Puppy-proof one more area if chewing is targeting something specific.
3. Check your puppy's mouth briefly for anything unusual.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming all chewing is teething and missing a boredom/stimulation gap.
- Punishing chewing after the fact.
- Not checking for retained baby teeth as permanent teeth come in.

## When to Call Your Veterinarian

Excessive bleeding, facial swelling, refusal to eat, a baby tooth clearly still present alongside an erupting permanent tooth (retained tooth — needs extraction to prevent crowding),<sup>4</sup> or any mouth discomfort well beyond typical mild teething signs.
