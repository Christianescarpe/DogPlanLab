<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Enrichment means giving your puppy genuine opportunities to use their mind — scent work, problem-solving, novelty, appropriate independence — distinct from physical exercise, not a substitute for it.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## What Enrichment Actually Means

Opportunities for natural, species-typical behaviors — sniffing, foraging, problem-solving, appropriate chewing — rather than a passive environment. See [Exercise vs. Mental Stimulation](/puppy-exercise/vs-mental-stimulation/) for the evidence behind this being distinct.

## Practical Categories

Scent-based activities; food-based problem-solving (see [Food Enrichment](/puppy-enrichment/food-enrichment/)); training as enrichment (see [Puppy Training](/puppy-training/)); novelty (new places, toy rotation); appropriate independence (see [Teaching Independent Play](/puppy-enrichment/independent-play/)).

## If Boredom Seems to Be the Actual Problem

See [Puppy Boredom](/puppy-enrichment/boredom/) for the diagnostic angle, and existing [Puppy Chewing Everything](/puppy-chewing/everything/) if destructive chewing is the symptom.

## Quick Reference

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Scent-based | Sniffing walks, treat-scatter, nose-work games |
| Food-based problem-solving | Puzzle feeders, snuffle mats, lick mats, stuffed toys |
| Training | Short sessions, new skills |
| Novelty | New environments, rotated toys |
| Independence | See [Teaching Independent Play](/puppy-enrichment/independent-play/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Treating enrichment as optional.
- Never rotating toys.
- Assuming physical exercise alone covers this need.
