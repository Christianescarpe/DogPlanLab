<AnswerFirstSummary>
  This popular rule is not traceable to any veterinary orthopedic organization, peer-reviewed study, or major veterinary body — it's a widely repeated internet rule of thumb, and even sources repeating it often admit there's no exact science behind it.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Where the Rule Actually Comes From

No traceable veterinary orthopedic society, peer-reviewed study, or AVMA/AAHA position supports this specific formula. It functions as informally circulated trainer/breeder folklore. One source repeating it states directly: "there is no exact science on the optimum amount of time puppies should be walked for."<sup>1</sup>

## What Genuinely Concerning Exercise Looks Like

Real, if less precise, veterinary caution exists around forced, repetitive, high-impact activity in young growing puppies: forced long-distance running, repeated hard-surface jumping, excessive stairs — especially in larger and giant breeds with a longer growth period.

## A Genuinely Interesting Counter-Finding

A controlled study of young Beagles jogging on a treadmill (moderate pace/incline, 15 weeks) found no cartilage damage — and measurable positive changes (increased cartilage stiffness and thickness).<sup>2</sup> A single controlled study in one breed, not a blanket claim — but real counter-evidence that moderate exercise isn't inherently harmful. The concern is specifically forced, repetitive, high-impact activity, not moderate activity broadly.

## What to Watch For Instead of Counting Minutes

**Signs of too much exercise:** limping/stiffness/reluctance after activity; excessive panting beyond what the activity explains; slower-than-usual recovery; reluctance to engage in a normally-enjoyed activity next time.

**Signs your puppy might need more activity:** excess energy consistently showing as destructive behavior/restlessness/difficulty settling; seeking constant engagement despite reasonable rest (see [Overtired Puppy](/puppy-sleep/overtired/) and [Puppy Enrichment](/puppy-enrichment/) before assuming "more exercise" is the answer).

## A Practical Way to Decide, Without a Formula

1. Favor self-directed, varied activity over forced repetition.
2. Watch recovery — bouncing back in a reasonable time beats any fixed number.
3. Avoid forced repetitive high-impact activity regardless of age.
4. Large/giant breed or specific orthopedic concern — ask your vet directly.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the rule as medical fact.
- Swinging to "exercise amount doesn't matter at all."
- Ignoring genuine caution around forced repetitive high-impact activity.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Limping, persistent stiffness, reluctance, or behavior change following activity.
