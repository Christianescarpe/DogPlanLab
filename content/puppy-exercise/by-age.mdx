<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Exercise appropriateness shifts along real developmental lines — coordination/stamina, growth-plate maturity (longer in larger breeds), and behavioral readiness — described qualitatively rather than by manufactured precision.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Not Week-by-Week Minutes

This project already found, for the Schedule cluster, that manufacturing precise weekly distinctions where evidence doesn't support them produces doorway pages. Same reasoning applies here — see [The Five-Minute Rule](/puppy-exercise/five-minute-rule/).

## What Genuinely Changes

**Very young (~8–12 weeks):** Short self-directed bursts, lots of rest, minimal structured expectation. Fit exercise around daytime naps and meals using our [puppy schedule by age](/puppy-schedule/by-age/).

**Older puppies (~3–6 months):** More stamina/coordination, still growing — continue favoring moderate over forced high-impact, especially in larger breeds.

**Approaching adolescence (6+ months):** Stamina often outpaces coordination/judgment — exactly when owners are tempted to increase intensity fastest.

## Breed/Size Matters More Than Age Alone

Larger and giant breeds have a longer growth period; orthopedic caution is more often emphasized for these breeds. No precise breed-by-breed numeric guidance is offered — talk to your vet directly if you have a large or giant breed puppy.

## Quick Reference

| Stage | General approach |
|---|---|
| ~8–12 weeks | Short, self-directed bursts; lots of rest |
| ~3–6 months | More stamina, still growing; moderate, varied activity |
| 6+ months | Stamina often outpaces judgment; keep favoring moderate |
| Larger/giant breeds, any age | Extra caution warranted — ask your vet |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a fixed minute count at any age.
- Increasing intensity just because stamina increased.
- Treating small and giant breeds the same.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Limping, reluctance, or behavior change after activity at any stage.
