<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Reluctance usually has an identifiable cause — temperament, environment, weather, or (less often but importantly) pain/illness — and ruling out the medical possibility matters before assuming it's purely behavioral.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Common Non-Medical Causes

Individual temperament/breed variation; environmental overwhelm (see existing Socialization content); weather discomfort (see existing weather guidance); simple lack of motivation.

## When to Consider a Medical Cause

A puppy normally willing to move who suddenly isn't, tires unusually fast, lags behind, or sits/lies down mid-walk deserves a veterinary conversation before continued assumption it's behavioral.

## Quick Decision Guide

| What you're seeing | Likely explanation | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Consistently lower energy, otherwise happy | Individual temperament | Adjust expectations |
| Reluctant in new/busy environments | Overwhelm | Build up gradually — see Socialization |
| Reluctant in specific weather | Weather discomfort | See weather guidance |
| Sudden change from willing to reluctant | Possible medical cause | Veterinary evaluation |
| Sits/lies down mid-walk | Possible medical cause | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming reluctance is "just stubborn."
- Forcing continued activity through apparent discomfort.
- Not considering a sudden-change medical explanation.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Sudden willingness change, limping, lagging, sitting/lying down mid-activity.
