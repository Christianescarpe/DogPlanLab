<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Ear handling follows the same gradual, reward-paired approach as other handling — but sudden new ear sensitivity deserves a veterinary look before assuming it's purely behavioral.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Building Comfort

1. Brief touch to the outer ear, paired with a reward.
2. Gradually include gently lifting the flap to look inside, without inserting anything.
3. Build toward brief handling resembling a vet exam or cleaning routine.
4. If starting from an aversion, begin more conservatively.

## Why Ears Deserve Extra Care in the Read

Sudden ear sensitivity is a common early sign of a genuine problem — worth ruling out before assuming it's purely comfort-related, especially if new.

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild head tilt/pull away, otherwise normal | Unfamiliarity | Continue gradual practice |
| Persistent flinching, shaking head, scratching | Possible irritation/infection | Veterinary evaluation |
| Odor, discharge, redness | Possible ear infection | Veterinary evaluation |
| Sudden new sensitivity, no other signs | Could be either | Veterinary evaluation first |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming all sensitivity is behavioral.
- Inserting anything into the canal.
- Continuing practice through genuine irritation.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Odor, discharge, redness, persistent shaking/scratching, apparent pain.
