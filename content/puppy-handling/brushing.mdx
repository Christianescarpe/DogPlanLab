  Introduce the brush itself before brushing, then build duration gradually — same principle whether starting fresh or working through dislike.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Building Comfort

1. Let your puppy investigate the brush first, paired with a reward.
2. Brief, gentle strokes in a comfortable area, paired with rewards.
3. Gradually extend to less-comfortable areas once easy ones are solid.
4. Keep early sessions short.

## If Your Puppy Already Dislikes It

Start much easier than whatever's triggering resistance and rebuild gradually.

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Wiggling, mild avoidance | Unfamiliarity | Continue gradual practice |
| Grabbing/biting at the brush | Often play/overstimulation, sometimes discomfort | Redirect if playful; shorten sessions if discomfort-driven |
| Stiffening, trying to flee | Fear response | Slow down; see [Puppy Fear](/puppy-fear/) |
| Yelping/flinching at a spot | Tangle/mat, or skin sensitivity | Check for mats; vet check if pain seems present |

## Common Mistakes

- A full thorough brush-out before any comfort exists.
- Pulling through mats forcefully.
- Only brushing when already matted.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Yelping, flinching, apparent pain at a spot without an obvious tangle.
