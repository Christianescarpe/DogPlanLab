<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Paws are commonly sensitive — build comfort gradually with brief touches paired with rewards, whether starting fresh or working through an existing aversion; the technique is the same, just paced differently.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Paws Get Extra Attention

Nail trims, wiping muddy feet, and vet exams all require paw tolerance.

## Building Comfort

1. Brief touch to one paw, paired with a treat, then release.
2. Gradually increase duration and add gentle pressure between toes.
3. If starting from an aversion, begin even more conservatively.
4. Practice with each paw.
5. Once comfortable, introduce nail-trimming tools as a separate step (see [Puppy Nail Trimming](/puppy-handling/nail-trimming/)).

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild pulling away, otherwise relaxed | Unfamiliarity | Continue gradual practice |
| Stiffening, trying to escape | Fear response | Slow down; see [Puppy Fear](/puppy-fear/) |
| Mouthing/biting when paws touched | Often overstimulation, not aggression | Use the standard biting response (see [Puppy Biting When Picked Up](/puppy-biting/when-picked-up/)); shorten sessions |
| Sudden new sensitivity, one paw specifically | Possible pain | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Only handling paws right before a trim.
- Restraining more firmly when a puppy pulls away.
- Treating all reluctance the same.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Sudden, localized sensitivity; limping; swelling, discharge, or odor.
