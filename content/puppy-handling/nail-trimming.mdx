<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Build paw comfort first, then introduce the clipper/grinder as a separate, no-pressure step before ever trimming — rushing to trimming before either foundation is solid is the most common reason nail trims become a fight.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## The Order Matters

1. General paw comfort (see [Touching Your Puppy's Paws](/puppy-handling/paws/)).
2. Tool introduction — investigate the clipper/grinder, paired with rewards, no touching yet.
3. Touching the tool to a nail without cutting, paired with a reward.
4. Trimming just the tip of one nail, then stopping — build across sessions, not all nails at once.

## Avoiding the Quick

The quick is the blood vessel inside the nail — cutting into it hurts and bleeds. Trim conservatively; light-colored nails show the quick as a pinkish area, dark nails make this harder, so trim more conservatively there.

## If You Do Nick the Quick

It will likely bleed and your puppy may yelp — startling, not usually an emergency. Apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth; styptic powder helps if you have it. Stay calm.

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Pulls paw away, otherwise tolerates | Normal caution | Continue gradual practice |
| Trembling, fleeing at sight of clipper | Fear of the tool | Go back to tool-introduction, more slowly |
| Biting/mouthing during trims | Often overstimulation, sometimes learned association | Use standard biting response (see [Puppy Biting When Picked Up](/puppy-biting/when-picked-up/)); shorten sessions |
| Consistently over-length nails | Practical/frequency issue | See [How Often Should a Puppy Be Groomed?](/puppy-handling/how-often/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Trimming all nails before any tolerance is built.
- Cutting quickly and risking the quick.
- Skipping tool introduction.
- Reacting with alarm if nicked.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Bleeding that doesn't stop within a few minutes; a cracked/split nail.
