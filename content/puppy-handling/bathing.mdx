<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Keep first baths short, calm, lukewarm, using dog-specific shampoo — build up gradually if your puppy is already anxious, since rushing a full bath before any comfort exists tends to create exactly the aversion you're avoiding.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Dog-Specific Shampoo Matters

Dog skin has a different pH balance than human skin; human shampoo can disrupt the protective skin barrier.

## Building Comfort

1. Get your puppy comfortable in/near the bath space first, dry, with rewards.
2. Introduce lukewarm water gradually, paws/legs before the rest.
3. Keep first several baths brief and simple.
4. Reward throughout.
5. If already anxious, go back to the dry step and rebuild.

## Managing the Actual Bath

Lukewarm water, avoid eyes/ears, dry calmly afterward.

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild squirming, tolerating it | Normal adjustment | Continue at current pace |
| Trying to escape, shaking, tucked posture | Fear response | Slow way down; rebuild from dry step |
| Persistent scratching, redness, odor after bathing | Possible skin irritation/product reaction | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Human shampoo.
- A long thorough first bath.
- Water in ears/eyes.
- Rushing a frightened puppy.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Persistent skin irritation, redness, or odor after bathing.
