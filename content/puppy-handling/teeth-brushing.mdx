## Never Use Human Toothpaste

Human toothpaste can contain xylitol, extremely toxic to dogs, capable of causing dangerously low blood sugar, seizures, or liver failure even in small amounts.<sup>1</sup><sup>2</sup> It can also carry high sodium and, with fluoride, repeated small exposures can build toward toxicity.<sup>2</sup> Use toothpaste made specifically for dogs.

## Building the Routine

1. Build general mouth comfort first (see [Mouth Handling](/puppy-handling/mouth/)).
2. Let your puppy taste dog-specific toothpaste from your finger.<sup>3</sup>
3. Introduce a finger brush or soft puppy toothbrush, a few teeth at a time.
4. Gradually cover more teeth, longer sessions.

## Realistic Frequency

Daily brushing is most effective, but several times a week is a realistic, still meaningfully effective alternative.<sup>4</sup> Consistent beats occasional "perfect."

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild resistance, otherwise tolerates | Normal adjustment | Continue gradual practice |
| Won't accept the toothpaste taste | Flavor mismatch | Try a different dog-specific flavor |
| Fights/bites during brushing | Often rushed introduction, sometimes discomfort | Go back to the finger-taste step; see [Puppy Biting When Picked Up](/puppy-biting/when-picked-up/) if it continues |
| Bad breath, red/swollen gums, reluctance to eat | Possible oral health issue | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Human toothpaste under any circumstance.
- Starting with a full session before comfort is built.
- Giving up after one resistant session.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Human toothpaste ingestion — contact your vet or a pet poison hotline. Bad breath, gum redness/swelling, bleeding, reluctance to eat.
