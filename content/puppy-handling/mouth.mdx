<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Building comfort with lips and mouth being gently touched supports vet exams, giving medication, and eventual teeth brushing — build it separately from the brushing routine itself.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Practice This Separately From Brushing

Mouth comfort is a prerequisite for many things beyond brushing.

## Building Comfort

1. Gently touch the outside of the lips, paired with a reward.
2. Gradually progress to briefly lifting the lip to see teeth/gums, without prying open.
3. Keep sessions very short.
4. Once comfortable, introduce teeth brushing as a separate step (see [Teeth Brushing](/puppy-handling/teeth-brushing/)).

## Troubleshooting

| What you're seeing | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Mild pulling away, otherwise relaxed | Unfamiliarity | Continue gradual practice |
| Stiffening or escaping | Fear response | Slow down; see [Puppy Fear](/puppy-fear/) |
| Mouthing/biting during handling | Often discomfort/overstimulation | Use standard biting response (see [Puppy Biting When Picked Up](/puppy-biting/when-picked-up/)) |
| Bad breath, redness/swelling, reluctance to eat | Possible dental/oral issue | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Prying the mouth open by force.
- Only handling right before brushing/a vet visit.
- Conflating mouth comfort with the brushing routine.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Bad breath, visible redness/swelling, bleeding, reluctance to eat.
