<AnswerFirstSummary>
  High-energy activity right before bed tends to backfire, while calmer mental engagement closer to bedtime supports settling better — timing and intensity matter more than the specific activity choice.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Timing Matters More Than the Activity Itself

Physical arousal doesn't switch off instantly — a vigorous session right before bed can leave a puppy wound up. This is why existing [Evening Routine](/puppy-schedule/evening-routine/) recommends the last high-energy activity happen well before bedtime, with a real wind-down after.

## What Works Well Closer to Bedtime

A lick mat (see [Food Enrichment](/puppy-enrichment/food-enrichment/)) — repetitive licking tends to be calming; a calm chew, not tug/fetch; gentle scent activity, slow and low-key.

## What to Avoid Right Before Bed

High-energy physical play; exciting fast-paced games right up against bedtime; a brand-new exciting enrichment item right before you need settling.

## How This Fits Into the Full Evening

This page covers the specific timing question; the complete wind-down sequence lives at existing [Evening Routine](/puppy-schedule/evening-routine/), not repeated here.

## Quick Reference

| Timing before bed | What to favor |
|---|---|
| 60–90 min | Last higher-energy activity (see [Evening Routine](/puppy-schedule/evening-routine/)) |
| 30–60 min | Begin winding down — calm mental engagement can start |
| Just before bed | Very calm only — lick mat, calm chew; nothing exciting/novel |

## Common Mistakes

- High-energy play right before bed.
- A new exciting item at bedtime.
- Assuming any "before bed" activity is fine regardless of intensity.
