<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Lack of interest can be normal temperament, trust/relationship building, the wrong type of play offered, or (less often but importantly) illness — ruling out the medical possibility matters, especially for a sudden change.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Common Non-Medical Causes

Individual temperament; trust/relationship still building (recently adopted); wrong type of play offered; overtiredness/overstimulation (see [Overtired Puppy](/puppy-sleep/overtired/)).

## When to Consider a Medical Cause

A previously playful puppy who's recently stopped, or general low energy beyond just toy disinterest, deserves a veterinary conversation.

## Quick Decision Guide

| What you're seeing | Likely explanation | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Never very toy-motivated, otherwise happy | Individual temperament | Try different play types |
| Recently adopted, still settling | Trust building | Give it time |
| Tired/overstimulated instead | Rest need | See [Overtired Puppy](/puppy-sleep/overtired/) |
| Sudden change from playful to disinterested | Possible medical cause | Veterinary evaluation |
| General low energy/withdrawal | Possible medical cause | Veterinary evaluation |

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing play.
- Assuming all disinterest is "just personality" without a sudden-change check.
- Not trying different play types.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Sudden change from playful to disinterested, or general withdrawal.
