<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Fetch is reasonable for most puppies, but the same caution around repetitive high-impact activity applies — moderate repetitions and intensity of stops/turns rather than throwing indefinitely.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Fetch Itself Isn't the Concern — Repetition Is

The caution applies to excessive repetition (many throws with hard stops/sharp turns/jumping), not fetch as a category.

## How to Play It Reasonably

Keep sessions moderate; watch for slowing recovery between throws; favor traction over slippery floors; moderate intensity for young/growing puppies rather than eliminating fetch.

## Combining With Training

Pairs naturally with impulse-control practice (see [Teaching Drop It](/puppy-training/drop-it/), [Training Around Distractions](/puppy-training/distracted/)).

## Quick Reference

| Situation | Reasonable approach |
|---|---|
| Occasional, moderate fetch | Generally fine |
| Extended, repetitive fetch to exhaustion | Worth moderating |
| Slippery indoor surface | Favor better traction |
| Young/growing puppy | Moderate intensity |

## Common Mistakes

- Extended repetitive sessions with no stopping point.
- Slippery surfaces.
- Ignoring slowing recovery.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Limping, stiffness, or reluctance following fetch specifically.
