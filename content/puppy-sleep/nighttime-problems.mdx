  There's no single fix, because "won't sleep" and "keeps waking up" usually come from the same handful of causes — adjustment, an elimination need, environmental disturbance, an off routine, discomfort, or (less often) something medical. What changes the answer: whether this is a new puppy still adjusting, whether a potty need is plausible given timing, and whether the pattern is improving or getting worse over time. If your puppy is specifically distressed *in a crate* (crying, panicking, escape attempts), go to [Puppy Crying in the Crate at Night](/crate-training/crying-at-night/) instead — that page owns the crate-specific decision framework in depth.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## First: Is This a Crate-Specific Problem?

If your puppy sleeps in a crate and the issue is specifically crying or visible distress tied to the crate itself, [Puppy Crying in the Crate at Night](/crate-training/crying-at-night/) has the full decision framework, including the immediate overnight action sequence. This page is for the broader case — a puppy who's restless, resists settling, or wakes repeatedly, whether crated, in a pen, or sleeping loose in a room.

## Why Puppies Have Trouble Settling or Wake Repeatedly

- **Adjustment.** A new environment takes time to feel normal — some difficulty settling in the first days/weeks is common and not a sign of a lasting problem.
- **Elimination need.** Especially for younger puppies, waking overnight is often genuinely about needing to go out — see [Potty Training](/potty-training/) for age-general context, though there's no universal formula for exactly how long a puppy can wait.
- **Environmental disturbance.** Noise, light, temperature, or an unfamiliar location can interrupt settling or cause waking.
- **Routine.** An inconsistent bedtime, too much stimulation right before bed, or skipped daytime naps can all affect nighttime sleep.
- **Discomfort.** An uncomfortable sleep setup, or (less commonly) a physical discomfort worth ruling out with your vet if the pattern doesn't fit anything else here.
- **Medical causes.** Less common, but real — see "When to Call Your Veterinarian" below.

## What to Check, in Order

1. **Could this reasonably be a potty need?** If it's been a while since the last trip out, or you're unsure, treat it as a potty need first.
2. **Has the routine changed recently?** New environment, skipped naps, a late/overstimulating evening — these are common, fixable culprits.
3. **Is the sleep environment consistent?** Same quiet, dim, comfortable spot each night.
4. **Is this new, or has it been going on for weeks without improvement?** New-puppy adjustment is expected to gradually get better; a pattern that isn't improving over a couple of weeks despite a consistent routine is worth a closer look.

## What Helps

- A calm, consistent wind-down routine before bed, rather than high-energy play right up until bedtime.
- Enforced daytime naps — a puppy who's genuinely well-rested during the day (not overtired, not understimulated) often settles better at night.
- Ruling out an obvious potty need before assuming it's something else.
- Consistency over several nights — a single rough night usually isn't meaningful; a pattern across a week or two is.

## If It's Not Improving

A pattern that persists despite a consistent routine and ruled-out potty needs for more than a couple of weeks is worth a closer look — either with your veterinarian (to rule out a medical cause) or, if it seems specifically tied to being alone rather than sleep itself, see [Puppy Home Alone](/puppy-home-alone/).

## Decision Table

| What you're seeing | Check first | If that's not it |
|---|---|---|
| Won't settle at bedtime | Was the evening overstimulating? Is the routine consistent? | Try an earlier, calmer wind-down for a few nights |
| Wakes once, seems to need to go out | Time since last potty trip | Take a calm, low-key potty break |
| Wakes repeatedly, multiple times a night | Environment (noise/light/temp), naps during the day | Address environment and daytime rest before assuming anything more serious |
| Crying/distress specifically tied to the crate | — | Go to [Puppy Crying in the Crate at Night](/crate-training/crying-at-night/) |
| No improvement after 2+ weeks of a consistent routine | — | Talk to your veterinarian |

## What to Do Today

1. Rule out an obvious potty need first.
2. Check whether the evening routine has gotten overstimulating.
3. Commit to a consistent, calm wind-down for the next several nights before concluding something's wrong.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming every nighttime waking is "just protest" without checking for a potty need.
- Adding more evening activity to try to tire a puppy out — often backfires.
- Giving up on a routine after one or two nights instead of a week or two.

## When to Call Your Veterinarian

A pattern that doesn't improve despite a consistent routine and ruled-out potty needs over a couple of weeks; any signs of discomfort, unusual vocalization, or physical symptoms alongside the sleep disruption.
