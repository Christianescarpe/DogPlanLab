<AnswerFirstSummary>
  No guaranteed age — depends on bladder development and routine consistency — many puppies manage a full night somewhere around 3 to 6 months, with real variation.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why This Varies So Much

Bladder capacity maturing and a settled overnight habit both have to align.

## What Genuinely Helps

Consistent bedtime routine (see [Puppy Evening Routine](/puppy-schedule/evening-routine/)); solid final potty trip; not reinforcing waking with exciting attention; patience.

## What Doesn't Reliably Speed It Up

Excessive evening water restriction (talk to your vet first); waking on a rigid schedule "to train" (see [Should You Wake a Sleeping Puppy to Potty?](/puppy-sleep/wake-to-pee/)).

## What to Expect, Roughly

| Stage | Reasonable general expectation |
|---|---|
| Under ~12 weeks | At least one overnight break typical |
| Middle months | Gradually longer stretches, real variation |
| ~3–6 months, consistent routine | Many manage a full night; range is wide |

*General pattern, not a promise.*

## Common Mistakes

- Expecting a specific age deadline.
- Assuming a setback means something's wrong.
- Restricting water without guidance.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Well past 6 months still needing frequent breaks with no improving trend — mention to your vet.
