<AnswerFirstSummary>
  No single evidence-backed correct answer — keeping a young puppy's sleep space near you initially is commonly recommended for practical reasons, and containment type is more a practical/safety tradeoff than a question with one right answer.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Location: Near You, at Least Initially

Practical reasons: hearing genuine needs, easing adjustment. Not a claim other locations are harmful.

## Containment: Crate, Pen, or Open Bed

Crate leverages the instinct not to soil the sleep space (see [Crate Training](/crate-training/) for setup). Pen offers more room, useful for an attached potty area. Open bed works once house training is established — before that, accidents can go unnoticed.

## A Note on Co-Sleeping

A personal household choice more than a training question — can make supervision harder and can be hard to change later; decide intentionally.

## Decision Guide

| Situation | Reasonable starting point |
|---|---|
| Very young, early house training | Crate or pen, bedroom-adjacent |
| Needs room but still confined | Pen with attached potty area if needed |
| House training reliable | Open bed/unconfined becomes reasonable |
| Considering co-sleeping | Decide intentionally |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming one universally correct setup.
- Moving unconfined before training is reliable.
- Defaulting into co-sleeping without considering tradeoffs.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Genuine distress tied to a specific setup — see [Puppy Crying in the Crate](/crate-training/crying/) — signals adjusting the setup, not pushing through.
