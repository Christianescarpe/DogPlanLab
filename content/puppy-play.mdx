<AnswerFirstSummary>
  No fixed "right" duration — watch arousal level and end the session before it tips from fun into overstimulation.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Duration Isn't the Right Question

Individual puppies vary enormously. Watching arousal trend is more useful than counting minutes.

## Reading Arousal During Play

Rising arousal is normal to a point — the skill is recognizing when it's climbing toward overstimulation. See existing [Puppy Body Language](/puppy-body-language/) for the framework.

## When to End a Session

Before your puppy stops responding to basic cues; if mouthing/biting escalates (see existing Biting content); if your puppy struggles to disengage.

## Puppy Won't Stop Playing / Seems Overexcited

Usually a sign the session should have ended earlier — end sessions proactively rather than trying to calm an already-overexcited puppy. See existing [Teaching Calm & Settle](/puppy-training/calm-settle/).

## If It's Dog-to-Dog Play

This page covers session management; for reading whether an interaction is healthy play, see existing [Play or Fighting](/puppy-body-language/play-or-fighting/), which this page doesn't repeat.

## Quick Reference

| Signal | What it suggests | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Engaged, responsive, loose body | Healthy play range | Continue |
| Escalating, less responsive | Approaching overstimulation | Wind down proactively |
| Mouthing/biting escalating | Past manageable arousal | Standard biting response; end session |
| Struggling to disengage | Overstimulated | End session, calm environment |

## Common Mistakes

- Playing to a fixed clock.
- Waiting until overexcited to end a session.
- Assuming dog-dog roughness equals human-directed roughness.
