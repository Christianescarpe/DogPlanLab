  No single signal reliably means one specific thing on its own; body language has to be read as a cluster, in context, against a baseline of what happened before and what happens next.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why Single Signals Mislead

"Tail wagging means happy" and "yawning means stress" are oversimplified. The same tail wag can accompany genuine excitement or real anxiety depending on the rest of the body and situation.

## The Actual Framework: Signal + Posture + Context + Before + After

1. **Signal** — the specific behavior (tail, ears, mouth, eyes).
2. **Posture** — the rest of the body (loose and wiggly, or stiff and rigid).
3. **Context** — what's actually happening around your puppy.
4. **Before** — what happened immediately before.
5. **After** — does your puppy settle, or escalate?

## A Note on Showing Teeth

A relaxed "submissive grin" (lips back, squinted eyes, wiggly body) is a distinct, generally non-aggressive display some dogs use during greetings — genuinely different from a stiff snarl with a hard stare.

## Quick Reference

| Question to ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What's the whole body doing, not just one part? | A single signal is easy to misread |
| What happened right before this? | Same behavior, different meanings by trigger |
| Is it settling or escalating? | Direction of change often matters more than the signal |

## Common Mistakes

- Reading one signal in isolation.
- Treating a popular one-line claim as settled science.
- Ignoring context.
