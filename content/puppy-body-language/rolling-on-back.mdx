<AnswerFirstSummary>
  "Submission" is an oversimplified, outdated explanation — a rolled-over posture can be a relaxed invitation in a comfortable moment, or an appeasement/"please stop" signal during an uncomfortable interaction — same posture, different meaning, depending on context.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why "Submission" Alone Doesn't Explain This

The traditional explanation comes from an older, simplified dominance-hierarchy model modern behavior science treats as overly simple.

## Two Very Different Contexts, Same Posture

- **Relaxed invitation:** calm play or approach for a belly rub, loose body, relaxed face — likely genuinely comfortable.
- **Appeasement/de-escalation:** rolling over when approached by an unfamiliar person/dog, with other stress signals present — more likely "please don't escalate," not an invitation.

## Why Getting This Wrong Matters

Reaching in for a belly rub during an appeasement/stress roll-over can increase discomfort rather than provide comfort.

## Quick Reference

| Context | Body otherwise | Likely meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Calm, familiar person | Loose, relaxed | Genuine invitation |
| Approached by someone new | Stiff, tucked tail, avoiding eye contact | Appeasement/stress, not invitation |
| During play with another dog | Loose, flips back quickly | Often normal play — see [Puppy Play or Fighting?](/puppy-body-language/play-or-fighting/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Automatically reaching for a belly rub regardless of context.
- Assuming "submission" fully explains it.
- Ignoring the rest of the body.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

A puppy rolling over as an appeasement response to an approaching stranger isn't necessarily asking for touch.
