<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Healthy play is loose, includes role reversals, self-handicapping, and voluntary pauses both dogs return from — a real fight tends to be one-sided, without pauses, with stiff and intense movement instead of loose and bouncy.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## What Healthy Play Actually Looks Like

- **Role reversals:** chaser becomes chased and back again.
- **Self-handicapping:** a bigger/stronger dog moderating their strength.
- **Play bow:** front down, rear up, often with a bouncy approach — a fairly reliable invitation signal.
- **Voluntary pauses and re-engagement**, both dogs choosing to continue.
- **Loose, exaggerated movement** vs. the tighter, efficient movement of real conflict.

## The "Pause Test"

Briefly interrupt the interaction. If both dogs want to re-engage, that's consistent with mutual play. If one dog uses the pause to leave and doesn't return, that's useful information.

## When Play Gets Too Rough

Watch for one dog consistently trying to disengage without success, body language shifting from loose to stiff, vocalizations shifting to genuine distress, or no self-handicapping from a size-mismatched pair. Interrupt and give both a break — not necessarily a "fight," just needs a reset.

## Play vs. Concerning

| Signal | Healthy play | Worth a closer look |
|---|---|---|
| Chasing | Switches back and forth | One-sided |
| Body | Loose, bouncy | Stiff, fast, tight |
| Pauses | Voluntary, both re-engage | One dog tries to leave |
| Size mismatch | Bigger dog self-handicaps | No moderation |
| Vocalization | Play growls with loose body | Paired with stiff, escalating body language |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming all growling during play is concerning (see [Is My Puppy Being Aggressive, or Just Playing?](/puppy-biting/aggression-or-play/)).
- Not intervening when one dog can't successfully disengage.
- Assuming one stiff moment means the whole interaction was a fight.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

One-sided pursuit without role reversal, a dog unable to disengage, or body language shifting to stiff and intense without resolving — separate the dogs rather than waiting.
