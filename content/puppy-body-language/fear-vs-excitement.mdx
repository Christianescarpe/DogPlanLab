<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Both are forms of arousal, and several signals overlap — the difference usually shows up in overall body posture (loose/forward-leaning vs. stiff/pulling away) and what your puppy does when given the option to approach or retreat.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why These Get Confused

Both involve genuine arousal, and panting, trembling, vocalizing, and jumping can all appear in either state.

## What Actually Distinguishes Them

| Signal | More consistent with excitement | More consistent with fear |
|---|---|---|
| Body orientation | Leaning toward | Leaning/pulling away |
| Movement | Loose, bouncy, forward | Stiff, retreating, frozen |
| Given the choice | Approaches, stays engaged | Moves away |
| Tail | Loose, sweeping | Tucked, low, or stiff/high and tight |
| Vocalization | Excited, with forward movement | Whining/barking with retreat |

## The Most Reliable Single Check

Give your puppy the choice — create space or an easy retreat path, and see what they do with it.

## Why Getting This Right Matters

Treating fear as excitement risks worsening a fear response by pushing forward.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming any single behavior settles the question.
- Not giving your puppy the option to approach/retreat.
- Pushing forward assuming excitement without checking.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

If genuinely unsure and another dog, child, or unfamiliar person is involved, treat cautiously as if it could be fear.
