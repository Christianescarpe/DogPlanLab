<AnswerFirstSummary>
  "Calming signals" was coined by a dog trainer, not originally scientific — the behaviors it describes are real and commonly seen around discomfort or uncertainty, but the theory that dogs deploy them deliberately to calm another party has only preliminary scientific testing.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Where the Term Came From

Turid Rugaas popularized it in her book describing roughly 30 behaviors, based on her own extensive observation as a trainer, not originally controlled research.<sup>1</sup>

## What's Been Tested Since

A peer-reviewed pilot study has examined whether these signals actually reduce aggression intensity during dog-to-dog interactions.<sup>2</sup> Being a pilot study, it's preliminary, not a settled conclusion.

## What This Means Practically

The individual behaviors are worth watching for. What's less settled is whether a dog is *intentionally signaling* versus simply *displaying* an involuntary response. This doesn't change what you should do (give space, reduce pressure) — only how confidently you should state the mechanism.

## The Common Behaviors Grouped Under This Term

- **Lip/nose licking** — often around uncertainty, but also just normal grooming.
- **Yawning** — can indicate stress, but obviously also just tiredness.
- **Turning head/body away** — commonly interpreted as de-escalation.
- **Moving in a curve** rather than straight at another dog or person.

## Using This Practically

Read alongside body posture and context — see [Puppy Body Language](/puppy-body-language/) for the full framework.

## Quick Reference

| Behavior | Commonly associated with | Also normal in other contexts |
|---|---|---|
| Lip/nose licking | Uncertainty, mild stress | Routine grooming |
| Yawning | Stress in a tense moment | Simple tiredness |
| Turning away | De-escalation attempt | Just looking at something else |

## Common Mistakes

- Presenting this as fully settled science.
- Treating a single yawn/lick as proof of distress without context.
- Ignoring the behaviors because the terminology is imperfect.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

If these signals cluster with other escalating stress signs, see [Signs of Stress](/puppy-body-language/stress-signals/) and [Puppy Fear](/puppy-fear/).
