<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Most of what changes between 8 and 10 weeks is minor; the more meaningful shift happens around 12 weeks, when teething, the vaccine series, and feeding frequency all start to transition at once. This describes general patterns, not a fixed timetable your puppy has to match — your individual puppy's development, breed size, and your veterinarian's guidance shape the specifics.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## 8–10 Weeks

- **Feeding:** Four meals a day is typical at this age.
- **Teeth:** Puppies at this age generally have their full set of baby teeth already in, with no active tooth loss yet — mouthing and chewing at this stage is about exploration and play, not new teeth coming in.
- **Elimination planning:** As a rough planning example (not a guarantee — this describes control right after eliminating specifically, and drinking, waking, or excitement can shorten it well below this), a puppy in this age range may manage on the order of a few hours between potty breaks right after emptying its bladder.
- **Vaccines:** Most puppies have had one dose of their core vaccine series by 8 weeks, with a second dose typically due in the 10–12 week window — meaning socialization at this stage should stay especially controlled (see the [Puppy Socialization Checklist](/puppy-socialization-checklist/) for specifics).
- **Sleep:** A very large portion of the day is still spent asleep; short awake windows between naps is normal, not a sign anything's wrong.

## 12 Weeks

- **Feeding:** This is around when some puppies begin transitioning from four meals to three, per general guidance — watch your puppy's cues and check with your vet on timing for your specific puppy.
- **Teeth:** Around 12 weeks is when permanent teeth generally begin erupting and baby teeth begin falling out — this is a genuinely more chewing-intensive, sometimes more uncomfortable phase than 8–10 weeks, and appropriate [chew toys](/crate-training/) become more important, not just nice-to-have.
- **Elimination planning:** As a rough planning example with the same caveats as above, a puppy around this age may manage on the order of an hour or so longer than the 8–10 week example, right after eliminating specifically.
- **Vaccines:** Many puppies are further into their vaccine series by 12 weeks (and leptospirosis, if your vet includes it, doesn't start before 12 weeks) — but "further into the series" isn't the same as "fully protected." Confirm with your vet before assuming lower-risk venues are appropriate.
- **Training:** Slightly longer, more structured training sessions are often realistic by this point, compared to the very short bursts that work best at 8–10 weeks.

## What This Means for Your Daily Schedule

The shape of the day (wake → potty → eat/play → activity → nap → repeat, as covered in the [Daily Puppy Schedule](/puppy-schedule/)) doesn't change dramatically between 8 and 12 weeks — what changes is the specifics within that shape: fewer, slightly longer stretches between naps; a possible feeding-frequency adjustment; more tolerance for structured training; and, around 12 weeks specifically, more deliberate management of teething/chewing.

## What to Do Today, Regardless of Exact Age

1. Confirm your puppy's specific vaccine timeline with your vet — general guidelines above are a planning reference, not a substitute for your puppy's actual schedule.
2. If you're near the 12-week mark and haven't already, ask your vet whether it's time to start transitioning meal frequency.
3. Keep appropriate chew toys accessible, especially from 12 weeks onward.
4. Don't treat any of the "planning example" numbers above as a guarantee — watch your actual puppy's signals over relying on the clock.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming a puppy "should" be able to hold it for a specific number of hours because of its age, and treating an accident as a failure when it happens sooner than that.
- Skipping ahead to fewer meals or longer training sessions because of age alone, rather than the individual puppy's readiness.
- Ignoring increased chewing around 12 weeks as "just being bad" instead of recognizing it as a genuinely more intense teething phase.

## When to Call Your Veterinarian

Confirm your puppy's specific vaccine schedule and any feeding-transition timing directly with your vet — these vary by individual puppy, breed, and your vet's clinical judgment. A puppy that seems excessively lethargic (well beyond typical napping) or is chewing to the point of injury to its gums warrants a vet check rather than assuming it's ordinary teething.
