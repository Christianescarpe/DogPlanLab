  A handful of items are genuinely necessary before your puppy comes home (crate or containment, food/water bowls, an ID tag, appropriate food); several more are useful for most households but not strictly urgent; and a few commonly-listed items are reasonably skippable until you know your specific puppy's needs.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Use This List

Prioritize by the categories below rather than buying everything on a generic checklist at once — several "essentials" you'll see listed elsewhere are really about a specific challenge you may or may not have yet.

## Must Have (Before Your Puppy Comes Home)

<Checklist items={[
  "Crate or other primary containment — see Crate vs. Playpen and Best Puppy Crates",
  "Food and water bowls",
  "Age-appropriate puppy food, as recommended by your breeder/shelter/vet — this site doesn't make specific brand/formula recommendations",
  "Collar and ID tag",
  "Leash",
  "A few appropriate chew toys — see Best Chew & Teething Toys",
  "Enzymatic cleaner — see Best Enzymatic Cleaners (accidents will happen regardless of preparation)",
  "Poop bags"
]} />

## Useful for Many Households

<Checklist items={[
  "Playpen, if your setup benefits from one alongside or instead of a crate — see Crate vs. Playpen",
  "Baby gate(s) for managing household access",
  "A dog bed appropriate to your containment choice",
  "Harness, as an alternative or addition to a collar for leash walks",
  "Training treats and a treat pouch",
  "At least one enrichment item — see Best Puppy Enrichment Toys",
  "Basic grooming tools appropriate to coat type — see existing Puppy Handling: Brushing",
  "A puppy-safe toothbrush and toothpaste — see existing Puppy Handling: Teeth Brushing (human toothpaste is genuinely unsafe, not just suboptimal)"
]} />

## Optional

<Checklist items={[
  "A puppy camera, if you want visual check-ins during confinement or alone-time practice",
  "Multiple enrichment item types — start with one category and expand as you learn what your puppy engages with",
  "A dedicated food-storage container"
]} />

## Skip / Not Needed Yet

<Checklist items={[
  "Multiple crates/beds for every room before you know your puppy's actual patterns",
  "Advanced or expert-level puzzle toys before your puppy has any puzzle-feeding experience",
  "A large wardrobe of toys bought all at once — rotation matters more than volume, see existing Puppy Enrichment",
  "Training tools this site doesn't recommend at any stage (shock collars, prong collars, choke chains) — see existing Puppy Training for the reward-based standard this site holds throughout"
]} />

## Common Mistakes

- Buying the full "useful for many" and "optional" tiers all at once instead of starting with must-haves and adding as real needs become clear.
- Assuming a longer checklist is automatically more prepared — several commonly-listed "essentials" elsewhere are genuinely optional.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Never substitute human products (toothpaste specifically) for pet-specific versions; the training-tools exclusion list above is a firm site-wide standard, not a preference.
