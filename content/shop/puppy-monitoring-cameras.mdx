## How to Choose

This is for anyone working through existing [Puppy Home Alone](/puppy-home-alone/) staged training, or the [Should You Wake a Sleeping Puppy to Potty](/puppy-sleep/wake-to-pee/) decision, who wants visual and audio confirmation rather than guessing.

## What to Compare

Night vision quality (most crate or alone-time monitoring happens in low light); two-way audio (lets you offer calm reassurance without entering the room, useful during early alone-time stages); app reliability and connection stability (a camera that frequently disconnects defeats the purpose); field of view appropriate to your crate or room size; whether local or cloud storage matters to you.

## Comparison

| Priority | Look for |
|---|---|
| Crate-specific monitoring | Compact size, close-range night vision, wide-enough field of view for a small space |
| Whole-room monitoring | Wider field of view, pan/tilt if available |
| Alone-time reassurance | Reliable two-way audio specifically |
| Budget-conscious | A basic night-vision-and-audio model without extra smart-home integrations you won't use |

## Best For / Not Ideal For

A camera is a genuinely useful information tool, not a behavioral fix — it won't resolve alone-time distress or the wake-to-pee tradeoff on its own. Use it alongside the existing staged training and decision content, not instead of it.

## FAQ

**Will a camera help me know if my puppy is genuinely distressed vs. just settling?** It gives you the information to observe, but interpreting what you see should still follow the distress-vs-adjustment framework in existing [Puppy Home Alone](/puppy-home-alone/) and [Puppy Body Language](/puppy-body-language/) content — the camera doesn't do that interpretation for you.

## Common Mistakes

- Relying on a camera instead of following the staged alone-time training approach.
- Choosing flashy features over reliable audio and connection stability.
- Interpreting normal settling behavior as distress without the body-language context.

Not directly applicable to this product category. Standard smart-device privacy and security practices — a strong password, updated firmware — apply as with any connected camera.
