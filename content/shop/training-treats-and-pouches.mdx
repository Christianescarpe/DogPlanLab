<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Trainers commonly emphasize that small, soft, fast-to-eat treats let you reward quickly without breaking training momentum — size and speed of consumption are widely treated as more important than flavor variety in trainer guidance we reviewed, though this reflects common practical wisdom rather than a claim we've verified against controlled research.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

This is for anyone actively using the reward-based method this site teaches throughout — see existing [Puppy Training: Rewards](/puppy-training/rewards/).

## What to Compare

Size (pea-sized or smaller for rapid-fire training reps); texture (soft, not crumbly — crumbs mean lost training time); ingredient simplicity (fewer, recognizable ingredients, particularly important for a puppy with any known sensitivity); one-handed pouch access; washable pouch material for a treat that will inevitably get sticky.

## Comparison

| Use case | Look for |
|---|---|
| General daily training | Small, soft, low-crumb treats |
| High-value or distraction-level rewards | A distinctly higher-value treat reserved specifically for harder training contexts — see existing [Training Around Distractions](/puppy-training/distracted/) |
| Sensitive stomach | Simple, limited-ingredient options |
| Carrying treats | A pouch with one-handed open/close and a washable liner |

## Best For / Not Ideal For

Large or hard treats are not ideal for rapid-fire training reps — they slow down the reward cycle and can be a choking risk if not sized down.

## FAQ

**Do treats need to be "high value" for everything?** No — reserve genuinely high-value treats for harder contexts, per existing distraction-training guidance, rather than using them for every rep.

## Common Mistakes

- Using large or hard treats that slow down rapid-fire training.
- Reserving high-value treats so rarely they lose their impact, or using them for every single rep.
- Choosing a pouch that requires two hands to open.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Treats still count toward daily caloric intake — see existing [Food Enrichment](/puppy-enrichment/food-enrichment/) for the same portion-awareness principle applied there. Avoid treats with ingredients your veterinarian has flagged for your specific puppy.
