<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Disposable pads are more convenient for genuinely temporary or high-volume use; washable pads cost less over time and reduce waste, but require consistent laundering — the right choice depends on your setup and how long you expect to use pads at all (see existing [Apartment Potty Training](/potty-training/apartment/) for the underlying decision of whether an indoor option makes sense for you in the first place).
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

This is for apartment or high-rise households, very young puppies not yet able to make it outside in time, or anyone using an indoor option as a deliberate bridge — not a permanent replacement — per existing guidance. This page assumes that decision is already made; see [Apartment Potty Training](/potty-training/apartment/) if it isn't yet.

## What to Compare

Absorbency and leak-proof backing; size relative to your puppy's current size (bigger than you think you need); scent-attractant claims (unverified marketing in most cases — treat with skepticism); for washable pads, machine-washable claims and expected lifespan; quantity and bulk economics for disposable.

## Comparison

| | Disposable | Washable |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Low | Higher |
| Convenience | Highest | Requires a laundering routine |
| Waste | More | Less |
| Best for | Temporary or travel use, very young puppies | Longer-term or repeated indoor use |

Cost comparisons here are directional, not calculated: disposable pads have a lower upfront cost but a recurring per-use cost; washable pads cost more upfront and are reused. We have not run the unit-cost math or verified current pricing to establish an actual break-even point — your real usage volume and willingness to launder regularly will matter more than any general cost claim.

## Best For / Not Ideal For

Disposable suits short-term or travel use. Washable suits a household committed to indoor options for longer, per existing guidance on the transition tradeoff.

## FAQ

**Will using pads make my puppy hard to switch to outdoor-only later?** See existing [Apartment Potty Training](/potty-training/apartment/) for the transition approach — not repeated here.

## Common Mistakes

- Buying based on scent-attractant marketing claims alone.
- Sizing too small for your puppy's current stage.
- Treating an indoor pad as a permanent solution without a plan to transition, if that's your actual goal.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Supervise a puppy who may attempt to chew or ingest pad material, and discontinue a specific product if this happens.
