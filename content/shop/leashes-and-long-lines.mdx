<AnswerFirstSummary>
  A standard 4-6 foot leash covers ordinary walks; a long line (15-30 feet) is a distinct tool for supervised recall practice and distance work in a safe, open area — not a replacement for a standard leash, and not appropriate for regular street walking.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

This is for anyone starting leash walking (see existing [Leash Training](/puppy-training/leash/)) or working on recall and distance skills in a safe setting.

## What to Compare

Material (nylon is standard and durable; leather is more expensive but often more comfortable over time); length matched to use case; clip and hardware durability; handle comfort; for long lines specifically, a lightweight material that won't tangle easily and a length matched to the space you'll actually use it in.

## Comparison

| Tool | Length | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| Standard leash | 4-6 ft | Everyday walks |
| Long line | 15-30 ft | Supervised recall or distance practice, open safe areas only |

## Best For / Not Ideal For

A long line is not appropriate for regular sidewalk or street walking — it's a training tool for controlled settings, not an everyday-walk replacement.

## FAQ

**Are retractable leashes ever appropriate for puppies?** This site does not recommend them for puppies still learning leash skills, based on the combination of general leash-injury data and manufacturer-acknowledged mechanism-specific risk described below.

## Common Mistakes

- Using a long line for regular street walking instead of a standard leash.
- Choosing a retractable leash for a puppy still learning leash manners.
- Sizing a leash or line for convenience rather than the setting you'll actually use it in.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Dog-leash-related emergency department visits in the U.S. rose from an estimated 25.4 to 105.5 per million population between 2001 and 2018, per a peer-reviewed analysis of national injury-surveillance data — fractures, sprains, and contusions from pulls and trip/tangle incidents were the most common injuries recorded. That data covers leashes generally, not retractable models specifically. Separately, a veterinary trade publication and at least one major retractable-leash manufacturer's own safety warnings describe a specific, elevated risk of laceration and finger amputation tied to the retractable cord or tape mechanism itself. This site does not recommend retractable leashes for puppies still learning leash manners, based on that combination of general leash-injury data and manufacturer-acknowledged mechanism-specific risk. A long line requires active management — watch for tangling around your own or your puppy's legs, or around other people or objects — and should not be used near traffic or in crowded areas.
