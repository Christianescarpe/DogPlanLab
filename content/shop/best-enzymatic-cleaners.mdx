  A true enzyme-or-bacteria formula, applied generously and left to sit (not wiped immediately), is the standard recommendation and works well for most household surfaces — though it's worth knowing that independent comparative testing has found some non-enzymatic oxidizer formulas perform just as well or better, so "enzymatic" alone isn't the only marker of an effective product.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

Match the product to your actual surface and situation — carpet/upholstery formulas, hard-floor formulas, and set-in-stain "soak" formulas aren't always interchangeable. Consider whether you want an unscented option (some puppies and owners are sensitive to strong citrus/fragrance additives) and whether you're buying for occasional use or genuine puppy-volume frequent accidents (a larger bottle or concentrate is more economical for the latter).

## What to Compare

Enzymatic vs. oxidizer formula type; surface compatibility (carpet, hardwood, upholstery); scent (unscented vs. fragranced); bottle size/concentration relative to expected use frequency; independent comparative test results where available, not just brand marketing claims.

### A Genuinely Important Finding From Independent Testing

Consumer Reports' own controlled testing of pet stain/odor sprays found that the top performers in blind testing weren't necessarily true enzyme formulas at all — oxidizer-based (hydrogen peroxide) formulas and certain plant-based surfactants performed comparably or better in their tests. This doesn't mean enzymatic formulas don't work — they clearly do, and remain a reasonable default — but it means "enzymatic" as a marketing label shouldn't be treated as the single deciding factor over actual test performance.

## Common Mistakes

- Wiping up a fresh spray immediately instead of letting it dwell per label instructions — this defeats the enzymatic action.
- Assuming a stronger smell means stronger cleaning power.
- Using a non-pet-specific household cleaner on urine, which doesn't address the underlying compounds the same way.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Check pet-safety and surface-compatibility claims directly on the product label before use, not just marketing copy; test an inconspicuous area first on delicate flooring or fabric; ventilate the area during and after use; keep puppies and children away from a treated area until fully dry, per standard label guidance; do not mix cleaning products.
