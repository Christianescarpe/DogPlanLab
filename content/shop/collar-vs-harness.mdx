<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Most puppies benefit from both — a well-fitted collar for holding an ID tag at all times, and a harness for the actual walking and leash-clipping connection, since a harness distributes pressure across the chest and body rather than the neck.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

This is for anyone deciding how to leash-walk a puppy and how to handle everyday ID-tag needs. For everyday collar or ID use, look for a comfortable, breakaway-safety-conscious fit that's adjustable for growth. For the actual walking connection, a well-fitted harness is the more commonly recommended default while your puppy is still learning leash manners — see existing [Leash Training](/puppy-training/leash/).

## Comparison

| Purpose | Use |
|---|---|
| Holding an ID tag at all times | A well-fitted flat collar |
| Everyday leash walking | A harness |
| A puppy still learning not to pull | Harness, not a collar — reduces neck-pressure risk during pulling |

## Best For / Not Ideal For

Clipping a leash directly to a collar for regular walks is not ideal for a puppy still learning leash manners, given the neck-pressure risk during pulling — a harness is the more commonly recommended default for this stage.

## FAQ

**Do I need both a collar and a harness?** Most households do — a collar for ID purposes worn at all times, a harness for the walking connection specifically.

## Common Mistakes

- Clipping a leash to a collar for daily walks while a puppy is still learning not to pull.
- Skipping a collar entirely because a harness is being used for walks.
- Not rechecking collar fit as your puppy grows.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

A collar should fit snugly enough not to slip over the head (the two-finger rule) but not so tight it restricts breathing or movement; re-check fit regularly as your puppy grows.
