<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Most households benefit from a crate for house-training's sake (the confined-space instinct discourages soiling) plus a playpen for supervised daytime containment with more room to move — many owners eventually use both, not one instead of the other.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## How to Choose

| If... | Favor |
|---|---|
| You're early in house training and want to leverage the den instinct | Crate (see existing [Crate Training](/crate-training/)) |
| You need a space with an attached potty area for longer stretches | Playpen, or a crate+playpen combination |
| Space is limited | Crate — smaller footprint |
| Your puppy needs room to move during supervised confinement | Playpen |
| You travel often | A collapsible crate travels more easily than most playpens |
| You want overnight containment | Crate is the more established choice for sleep (see existing [Where Should a Puppy Sleep](/puppy-sleep/where-should-puppy-sleep/)) |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming you must choose only one, forever.
- Buying a playpen expecting it to fully replace crate-based house-training benefits.
- Buying either before reading the sizing guidance.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Whichever you choose, never use containment as punishment (see existing [Crate Training](/crate-training/)); both crates and playpens require appropriate sizing — see [Puppy Crate Size Guide](/shop/crate-size-guide/) for the crate-specific version.
