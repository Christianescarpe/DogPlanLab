  Baby teeth erupt starting around 3 weeks and are usually complete by 6 weeks; permanent teeth begin replacing them around 12 weeks, with different tooth types erupting at different points through about 6 months. What changes the answer: individual and breed variation — this is a general timeline, not a guarantee for your specific puppy.

## The Full Timeline

| Age | What's Happening |
|---|---|
| ~3 weeks | Baby (deciduous) teeth begin erupting |
| ~6 weeks | Baby teeth usually fully in (28 total) |
| 8–10 weeks | Full set of baby teeth, no active eruption — typical age for coming home with a new owner |
| ~12 weeks | Permanent teeth begin erupting; baby teeth begin falling out — the more intense teething phase starts |
| ~3–5 months | Permanent incisors (front teeth) typically erupt |
| ~4–6 months | Permanent canines typically erupt |
| ~4–6 months | Permanent premolars typically erupt |
| ~6 months | Most puppies have a full set of permanent teeth (42 total) |

*Individual and breed variation is real — this table describes typical timing, not a guarantee.*

## Why the Tooth-Type Breakdown Matters

Teething isn't one single event — it's a series of overlapping eruptions across different tooth types over several months. That's part of why the most intense chewing phase (roughly 12 weeks to 6 months) can feel like it drags on: different teeth are coming in at different points throughout that whole window, not all at once.

## Watching for Retained Baby Teeth

As permanent teeth come in, the baby tooth in that spot should fall out around the same time. Sometimes it doesn't — a "retained" baby tooth sitting alongside an erupting permanent tooth can force the permanent tooth into an abnormal position, leading to crowding or bite problems if not addressed.<sup>1</sup> Checking your puppy's mouth weekly through about 7–8 months is a reasonable habit specifically to catch this.<sup>1</sup>

## What to Watch For, by Stage

- [ ] 8–10 weeks: full set of baby teeth, no action needed unless something looks abnormal
- [ ] 12 weeks onward: watch for any baby tooth still present next to a visibly erupting permanent tooth
- [ ] Ongoing through 7–8 months: weekly quick mouth checks
- [ ] 6+ months: confirm with your vet (often at a routine visit) that all permanent teeth are in and no baby teeth remain

## What to Do Today

1. Do a quick, gentle mouth check if your puppy is in or near the 12-week-plus window.
2. Note the date if you spot anything that looks like a retained tooth so you can mention it at the next vet visit.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming teething is "done" once the most obvious chewing phase eases, without checking that all baby teeth actually fell out.
- Not noticing a retained tooth until it's caused crowding.

## When to Call Your Veterinarian

Any baby tooth visibly still present alongside an erupting permanent tooth in the same location; teeth that look crowded or crooked as permanent teeth come in; any of the general concerning signs listed on the [Puppy Teething](/puppy-teething/) hub.
