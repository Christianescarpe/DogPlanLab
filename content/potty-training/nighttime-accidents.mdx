<AnswerFirstSummary>
  There's no reliable universal age by which a puppy "should" sleep through without an accident — management matters more than a milestone.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why We're Not Giving You an Age

Individual variation is real, and a promised milestone that doesn't match your puppy can create unwarranted worry.

## What Helps, Practically

- A consistent pre-bed routine, including a final calm potty trip.
- A sleep space sized appropriately.
- One calm, low-key overnight trip if your puppy seems to need it — minimal light, no play, straight back to bed.
- A predictable last-meal/last-water timing in the evening, without restricting water excessively — check with your vet if unsure.

## When to Expect Real Improvement

Look for a trend across a couple of weeks, not a single night.

## Troubleshooting

| Pattern | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Accident happens without waking/crying first | Likely a capacity issue, not defiance | Add a mid-night trip temporarily; reassess bedtime water timing |
| Accidents cluster right after extending overnight interval | Extended faster than capacity supports | Go back to the previous, working interval |
| Sleep space oversized | Reduced den-instinct effect | Check sizing |
| Nighttime accidents alongside daytime accidents too | Broader training gap | See [Potty Training](/potty-training/) and [Accidents](/potty-training/accidents/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Promising a specific age deadline.
- Restricting water excessively without veterinary guidance.
- Refusing overnight trips "to build independence faster."
- Extending duration faster than demonstrated capacity.

## Medical Red Flags

Frequent nighttime accidents that don't improve with a consistent routine over a couple of weeks, especially with increased daytime thirst/urination — see [Frequent Urination](/potty-training/frequent-urination/).
