  Crate size is one possible cause, not the automatic explanation — duration relative to capacity, incomplete house training, GI upset, bedding, prior kennel history, and distress can all cause crate accidents, and they call for different fixes.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Don't Jump Straight to "The Crate Is Too Big"

Work through the possibilities below before concluding it's a sizing issue.

## Possible Causes

- **Duration exceeds current capacity** — see [Crate Training Schedule](/crate-training/schedule/) for the staged, response-based approach.
- **Crate too large** — lets a puppy use one end as a bathroom, undermining the den instinct.
- **Incomplete house training generally** — if accidents happen outside the crate too, see [Potty Training](/potty-training/).
- **Bedding/substrate** — some materials feel similar enough to an absorbent surface to not discourage elimination.
- **Previous kennel/shelter history** — some puppies haven't yet developed the usual aversion to soiling their space.
- **GI upset or diarrhea** — a health question, not a training one.
- **Distress** — if paired with vocalization, panting, escape attempts, see [Puppy Crying in the Crate](/crate-training/crying/).

## A Practical Diagnostic Approach

1. Check crate sizing against your puppy's current size.
2. Compare crate duration to demonstrated capacity elsewhere in the day.
3. Check whether accidents are crate-only or happening everywhere.
4. Check stool quality if defecation is the issue.
5. Check for distress signs alongside the accidents.

## Troubleshooting

| Pattern | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Only in crate, otherwise reliable | Duration or sizing | Reassess crate duration against demonstrated capacity; check sizing |
| Everywhere, not just the crate | Incomplete general training | See [Potty Training](/potty-training/) |
| Loose stool, not just accidents | Possible GI upset | Talk to your veterinarian |
| Paired with distress | Distress, not (only) a potty issue | See [Puppy Crying in the Crate](/crate-training/crying/) |
| Recently adopted from a kennel/shelter | Prior soiling history | Rebuild den-instinct gradually, same as initial crate training |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming crate size is always the answer.
- Punishing a crate accident.
- Extending duration before capacity is demonstrated.
- Ignoring loose stool as "just an accident."

## Medical Red Flags

Diarrhea, straining, blood in urine or stool, or a sudden change with no obvious behavioral explanation — talk to your veterinarian.
