## Common Natural Signals

Circling/sniffing intently; sudden restlessness; heading toward the door; a sudden pause mid-activity; whining or pacing near the exit.

## Why Some Puppies Barely Signal at All

Signal clarity varies between puppies, and very young puppies especially may not signal much before an accident. This is why the trigger-time schedule (see [Potty Training Schedule](/potty-training/schedule/)) is the primary system, with signals as a helpful supplement.

## Teaching a Taught Signal: Potty Bells

Hang a bell at nose height near the door, ring it yourself every trip out, and reward accidental contact your puppy makes on the way out. Many puppies learn to ring it deliberately over time — not every puppy takes to it, and that's fine.

## What to Do

1. Watch for common natural signals during supervised time.
2. Don't rely on signals alone yet — keep the trigger-time schedule regardless.
3. If trying bell training, ring it yourself every trip and reward contact.
4. Log which signals your specific puppy actually shows.

If your puppy shows no signals at all even after weeks, lean more heavily on the schedule and closer supervision.

## Common Mistakes

- Waiting for a clear signal before taking a very young puppy out.
- Assuming no obvious signaling means something's wrong.
- Expecting bell training to work without consistent pairing.

## When to Take a Closer Look

Not directly medical, but if signals or accidents change suddenly, see [Regression](/potty-training/regression/) and [Frequent Urination](/potty-training/frequent-urination/).
