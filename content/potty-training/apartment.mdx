<AnswerFirstSummary>
  The core method is the same — the difference is logistics: longer travel time, shared-space disease risk, and a genuine decision about whether an indoor potty option makes sense.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## The Extra Time Factor

Elevator rides and walks to a suitable outdoor spot add minutes a very young puppy may not reliably have.

## Shared Spaces and Disease Risk

Building common areas see a lot of unknown-dog traffic — worth factoring into where you potty your puppy, especially before full vaccination (see [Socializing Before Full Vaccination](/puppy-socialization/before-vaccinations/)).

## The Indoor Option: A Real Tradeoff

Puppy pads or artificial turf are legitimate, especially early on. The tradeoff: a puppy who's learned indoor elimination as default may need a deliberate transition to outdoor-only later. Decide your long-term goal up front.

## Practical Adjustments

1. Build the elevator/travel-time buffer into your trigger-time schedule (see [Potty Training Schedule](/potty-training/schedule/)).
2. If using an indoor option, place it consistently.
3. If transitioning to outdoor-only, move the indoor spot progressively closer to the exit before removing it.
4. Carry your puppy through shared spaces during the pre-vaccination window.

## Troubleshooting

| Situation | What helps |
|---|---|
| Can't make it in time due to travel delay | Start the trip earlier; consider a temporary indoor option |
| Transitioning indoor to outdoor-only | Gradually relocate the indoor spot closer to the exit |
| Shared-space disease-risk concern pre-vaccination | Carry your puppy through common areas |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming an indoor option is always necessary or never appropriate.
- Switching abruptly without a gradual transition.
- Underestimating travel time.

## Medical Red Flags

Same general red flags as elsewhere in this cluster — straining, blood in urine or stool, or a sudden unexplained change are worth a veterinary visit.
