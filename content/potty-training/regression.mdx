</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Regression Is Common, Not a Failure

A sudden run of accidents after weeks of reliability is a very common report and doesn't mean the earlier training didn't work.

## What Commonly Triggers Regression

- A recent routine or environment change (move, schedule shift, new pet or family member, travel)
- Too much freedom too soon
- A growth-related capacity/independence mismatch
- Incomplete cleanup of an old accident
- A missed change in schedule (new caregiver, less consistent supervision)
- A medical cause (less common, but real — see red flags below)

## What to Do

1. Return to the supervision level that worked before, even if it feels like a step back.
2. Re-tighten the schedule (see [Potty Training Schedule](/potty-training/schedule/)).
3. Clean any recent accident spots thoroughly with an enzymatic cleaner.
4. Give it two weeks of consistent, tightened supervision before reassessing.

## Troubleshooting

| What changed recently | Likely trigger | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Moved / new environment | Adjustment | Re-tighten supervision and schedule temporarily |
| Given more freedom | Freedom outpaced reliability | Scale back supervision level |
| Nothing obvious changed | Possible medical cause, or an unnoticed change | See "Medical Red Flags" |
| Accidents cluster in a specific old spot | Incomplete cleanup | Clean thoroughly with enzymatic cleaner |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming regression means starting over from scratch.
- Punishing regression as defiance.
- Ignoring regression that doesn't improve after returning to tighter supervision.

## Medical Red Flags

A regression with no identifiable behavioral trigger, especially with straining, frequent small-amount urination, blood in urine, increased thirst, lethargy, or appetite changes, warrants a veterinary visit.
