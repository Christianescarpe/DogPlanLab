<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Excitement urination and submissive urination are both involuntary — not a house-training failure — and most puppies grow out of both as they mature.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## These Are Involuntary, Not a Training Problem

Veterinary behavior sources treat excitement and submissive urination as related, involuntary responses most common in young or socially inexperienced dogs. Punishing or scolding worsens submissive urination specifically, since it adds the same social pressure that triggers it.

## Excitement Urination

Happens during high-arousal moments — an exuberant greeting, energetic play, an owner's return. Tends to improve with age.

## Submissive Urination

Happens during moments of perceived social pressure — being approached directly, greeted by an unfamiliar person/dog, loomed over, or after a correction. A deference signal, not defiance.

## What Actually Helps

- Keep greetings low-key (crouch sideways, avoid direct eye contact, let your puppy approach).
- Don't punish or scold it.
- Take your puppy outside proactively before likely triggers.
- Build calm confidence generally through gentle socialization (see [Puppy Socialization Checklist](/puppy-socialization-checklist/)).

## Troubleshooting

| Pattern | Likely type | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| During energetic play or excited greetings | Excitement urination | Calmer arrivals/greetings; proactive outside trips |
| Specifically when approached, loomed over, or after correction | Submissive urination | Approach calmly, sideways, at puppy level; avoid corrections |
| Both contexts | Overlap is common | Apply both; lower overall arousal/pressure |

## Common Mistakes

- Scolding or punishing the urination itself.
- Treating this as a house-training failure.
- Looming over a puppy already showing submissive body language.

## Medical Red Flags

Frequent involuntary urination outside clear excitement/pressure contexts, or paired with straining, blood in urine, or increased general thirst — see [Frequent Urination](/potty-training/frequent-urination/).
