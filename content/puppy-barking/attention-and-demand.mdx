<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Barking or whining that reliably happens when your puppy wants attention or a specific outcome is being reinforced by getting a reaction — even a negative one — so the fix centers on withdrawing that reward and rewarding calm/quiet instead, applied consistently.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Attention-Seeking vs. Frustration

Attention-seeking is a general bid for engagement; frustration is wanting a specific outcome and being blocked from it right now. Both respond to the same core approach.

## Why This Works the Way It Does

A reaction — even scolding — reinforces the vocalization. The fix is consistently not rewarding the noise while consistently rewarding calm.

## The Core Approach

1. Identify what's actually being requested.
2. Don't reward the vocalization — no eye contact, no talking, no giving in while it's happening.
3. Reward the pause/quiet moment instead.
4. Be consistent across the household.

## A Note on What to Expect

A temporary increase in the behavior before it decreases — an "extinction burst" — is a well-documented general pattern. Don't read it as failure; giving in specifically because of this increase makes the pattern more persistent, not less.

## When Ignoring Isn't the Right Tool

Not for fear-driven barking, alone-time distress, or anything where the cause isn't "wants a reaction" — see [Should You Ignore Barking?](/puppy-barking/should-i-ignore-it/) for the fuller treatment.

## Troubleshooting Table

| Pattern | Likely driver | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Barks/whines when you stop playing | Attention-seeking | Withdraw attention for the noise, reward the pause |
| Barks/whines at a specific toy/door | Frustration | No access during the noise; reward calm, then grant access |
| Increases right after you start ignoring it | Extinction burst | Stay consistent |
| No improvement after 1–2 weeks | Worth a closer look | See [Puppy Won't Stop Barking](/puppy-barking/wont-stop/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Giving in "just this once."
- Inconsistent household responses.
- Mistaking an extinction burst for failure.
- Applying this to fear-driven or alone-time vocalization.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Vocalization escalating rather than settling despite consistent effort, or paired with genuine distress rather than simple demand.
