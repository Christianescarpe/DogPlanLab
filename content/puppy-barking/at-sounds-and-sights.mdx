<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Barking at ordinary sounds or window-visible movement is usually rehearsed alert behavior — managing access and rewarding an alternative usually helps more than reacting to each bark.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Why This Often Gets Worse Without Management

Every "successful" alert reinforces the pattern — lots of window repetition means lots of practice.

## What Helps

Reduce visual access; reduce unsupervised rehearsal time; teach and reward an alternative when a trigger appears; don't punish the alert itself.

## If It's a Specific Sound and Looks Like Fear

A puppy reacting to one specific sound with fear body language, not confident alert barking, is different — see existing [Scared of Noises](/puppy-fear/scared-of-noises/).

## Troubleshooting Table

| Pattern | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Barks at anything passing a window, stops once gone | Alert, well-rehearsed | Manage visual access; teach alternative |
| Barks at a specific recurring sound | Alert to a known trigger | Same approach, applied specifically |
| Fear body language toward one sound | Fear-driven | See [Scared of Noises](/puppy-fear/scared-of-noises/) |
| Barks at nearly everything, hard to redirect | Possibly high overall stimulation | See [Stress Signals](/puppy-body-language/stress-signals/) |

## Common Mistakes

- Unsupervised constant window access.
- Punishing normal alert behavior.
- Treating a sound-fear case with alert-management technique.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Escalating, intense reactions not improving with management, or genuine panic — see [Scared of Noises](/puppy-fear/scared-of-noises/).
