<AnswerFirstSummary>
  If consistent, correctly matched technique isn't working, common gaps are: the wrong cause was identified, inconsistency across the household, insufficient trigger management, or an unmet underlying need (exercise, stimulation) alongside the vocalization-specific training.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Check the Cause First

Barking that looks like demand but is actually fear-driven won't respond to attention-withdrawal — revisit [Why Is My Puppy Barking?](/puppy-barking/) and check body language (see [Puppy Body Language](/puppy-body-language/)).

## Check Consistency

One household member undoing another's approach undermines the pattern for everyone.

## Check Trigger Management

Training alone competes poorly against continuous rehearsal opportunities if the trigger stays fully accessible.

## Check Underlying Needs

A puppy without enough exercise/stimulation may bark more across many contexts — see [Daily Puppy Schedule](/puppy-schedule/), [Overtired Puppy](/puppy-sleep/overtired/).

## If You've Checked All of This

Give it real time. If genuinely no improvement after weeks of consistent, correct effort, a certified trainer observing in person is a reasonable next step.

## Troubleshooting Table

| What to check | Common finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cause correctly identified? | Fear mistaken for demand or vice versa | Revisit [Why Is My Puppy Barking?](/puppy-barking/) |
| Household consistency? | Inconsistency | Align the approach |
| Trigger still fully accessible? | Insufficient management | Reduce access alongside training |
| Enough exercise/stimulation generally? | Unmet broader needs | See [Daily Puppy Schedule](/puppy-schedule/) |
| No improvement after weeks | Needs in-person assessment | Certified trainer |

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming "nothing works" without checking the gaps above.
- Escalating to punishment.
- Giving up on consistency after a few days.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Barking increasing despite genuinely consistent, correctly matched effort.
