<AnswerFirstSummary>
  Barking at other dogs is often excitement or frustration rather than fear — read body language to tell them apart, since the fix genuinely differs.
</AnswerFirstSummary>

## Excitement/Frustration vs. Fear

Straining toward a dog, loose body, forward-leaning barking is frustration; pulling away, tucked tail, increasing-distance barking is fear (see [Scared of Dogs](/puppy-fear/scared-of-dogs/)).

## At Home: Window/Yard Barking

Manage visual access; teach and reward an alternative response; reward calm behavior specifically, not just quiet-but-staring.

## On Walks

1. Increase distance before your puppy reacts, not after.
2. Reward calm attention at a distance the trigger doesn't cause a reaction.
3. Gradually decrease distance only as calm holds (see [Training Around Distractions](/puppy-training/distracted/)).
4. Avoid forced greetings as a "fix."

## If It Looks Like Genuine Reactivity

Intense, non-improving, or worsening patterns over time warrant a certified trainer or veterinary behaviorist rather than continued DIY.

## Troubleshooting Table

| Pattern | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Straining toward the dog, loose body | Frustration/excitement | Increase distance, reward calm, decrease gradually |
| Pulling away, tucked tail | Fear-driven | See [Scared of Dogs](/puppy-fear/scared-of-dogs/) |
| Barks at every dog visible from a window | Alert/frustration | Manage visual access |
| Intense, escalating, not improving | Possible reactivity | Certified trainer/veterinary behaviorist |

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing a greeting.
- Assuming all dog-directed barking is the same.
- Decreasing distance faster than calm behavior supports.

## Safety and Behavior Red Flags

Intense lunging/barking not responding to distance management, or worsening over time.
