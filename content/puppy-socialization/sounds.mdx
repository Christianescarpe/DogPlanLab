## Gradual and Positive, Not Sudden and Full-Volume

The same principle used to treat existing noise fear applies just as well to preventing it in the first place: start at an intensity low enough that your puppy stays comfortable, and pair the sound with something good.<sup>1</sup> A vacuum cleaner heard from another room, then closer; a doorbell at low volume (many can be temporarily adjusted) before full volume; traffic sounds from a distance before walking close to a busy street.

## Common Sounds Worth Proactively Introducing

Vacuum cleaner, doorbell/knocking, traffic, household appliances, and — with real caution — thunder/fireworks-type sounds, ideally introduced calmly at very low volume well before an actual storm or holiday season, not for the first time during the real event.

## What NOT to Do

Don't wait for a real event (an actual thunderstorm, an actual fireworks show) to be your puppy's first exposure to that sound at full intensity — an uncontrolled first exposure is exactly the setup for lasting fear, not a neutral test.

## What to Do Today

1. Pick one sound to introduce this week.
2. Start at the lowest volume/greatest distance where your puppy stays relaxed.
3. Pair the sound with a treat, calm play, or a favorite activity.
4. Gradually increase volume/proximity across multiple short sessions, not all in one sitting.
5. If your puppy shows any sign of fear (freezing, trying to leave, cowering), you've moved too fast — go back a step.

If your puppy seems anxious even at a low starting volume, that's a signal to work with a professional rather than continuing to push forward on your own — see [Puppy Fear: Scared of Noises](/puppy-fear/scared-of-noises/).

## Common Mistakes

- Introducing a scary sound at full volume "to get it over with."
- Comforting with excessive petting/coddling in a way that reinforces anxious body language rather than calm behavior.
- Using a genuinely frightening real-world event (an actual thunderstorm) as the first-ever exposure.

## When Professional Help May Be Appropriate

If your puppy already shows fear of a specific sound rather than simple mild startle, this proactive page isn't the right tool — [Puppy Fear: Scared of Noises](/puppy-fear/scared-of-noises/) covers the structured desensitization approach that existing fear requires, ideally with a qualified trainer or behaviorist involved for moderate-to-severe cases.<sup>1</sup>
